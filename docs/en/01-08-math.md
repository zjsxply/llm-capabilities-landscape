# 1.8 Mathematics (Including Multimodal Mathematics)

> Parent chapter: 1. Foundational Capabilities


## 1.8.1 Leaderboard

- [LiveBench Mathematics](https://livebench.ai/) ([code](https://github.com/LiveBench/LiveBench)): The mathematics sub-leaderboard in LiveBench updates its questions over time, making it suitable for comparing mathematical capability within a broader general-model leaderboard.
- [FrontierMath / Epoch Benchmarking Hub](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/frontiermath): An expert-level mathematics leaderboard maintained by Epoch AI, including private sets, public sample questions, and a log viewer; especially suitable for observing difficult mathematical solving settings with executable Python and formal answer functions.
- [MathArena](https://matharena.ai/?view=problem) ([model page](https://matharena.ai/models?page=1&q=); [output data](https://huggingface.co/MathArena)): One of the most practical continuous competitive-math leaderboards today, publishing problem-level scores, raw outputs, and model-level aggregate results for tasks such as AIME, HMMT, USAMO, IMO, Project Euler, and Apex.

## 1.8.2 Bench

Note: Mathematical evaluation for current reasoning models has clearly split into two main lines: `informal mathematical answering`, which emphasizes final answers or readable solution traces, and `formal proof`, which emphasizes proof-assistant verifiability and reproducible runtimes. Multimodal mathematics further incorporates problem statements and diagram information into the input, requiring OCR/parsing/diagram reasoning to work with symbolic computation.

- [MATH](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.03874) ([Papers with Code MATH Leaderboard](https://paperswithcode.com/sota/problem-solving-on-math); [code](https://github.com/hendrycks/math)): What it evaluates: solving competition mathematics problems; core idea: broader coverage and higher difficulty, often paired with subsets such as `MATH-500` for quick comparison, making it suitable for characterizing improvements of reasoning models at competition difficulty.
- [miniF2F](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.00110) ([code](https://github.com/openai/miniF2F)): What it evaluates: formal olympiad proofs across Lean, Metamath, Isabelle, and HOL Light; core idea: unify formal proofs into verifiable goals, suitable for evaluating end-to-end autoformalization and formal proving.
- AIME 2024 ([AIME I dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/MathArena/aime_2024_I); [AIME II dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/MathArena/aime_2024_II)): What it evaluates: short-answer competition mathematics problems from AIME; core idea: while maintaining high discrimination, earlier years are often used for alignment with prior work, though they are more vulnerable to training-data coverage.
- [AIME 2025](https://huggingface.co/datasets/MathArena/aime_2025): What it evaluates: short-answer AIME competition mathematics problems, with 15 questions each year for I and II; core idea: high difficulty, short statements, and strong discrimination have made it a frequent benchmark in reasoning-model reports.
- [AIME 2026](https://huggingface.co/datasets/MathArena/aime_2026): What it evaluates: the same category as above; core idea: use more recent real exam problems to reduce contamination risk and often test true generalization rather than memorized problems.
- [HMMT Feb 2025](https://huggingface.co/datasets/MathArena/hmmt_feb_2025) ([official archive](https://www.hmmt.org/www/archive/2025/feb/)): What it evaluates: competition mathematics problems from HMMT February; core idea: complements AIME with a different type and difficulty distribution, providing stronger discrimination and a stress test closer to team-contest or comprehensive-problem style.
- [HMMT Nov 2025](https://huggingface.co/datasets/MathArena/hmmt_nov_2025) ([official archive](https://www.hmmt.org/www/archive/2025/nov/)): What it evaluates: competition mathematics problems from HMMT November; core idea: same as above, using more recent contest problems as an anti-contamination supplement.
- [HMMT Feb 2026](https://huggingface.co/datasets/MathArena/hmmt_feb_2026): What it evaluates: the February 2026 HMMT competition problem set. Core idea: keep the HMMT-style competition-math pressure point current, matching the recent contest variants highlighted in Kimi K2.6 and other frontier model cards.
- USAMO 2026 (reported in the [Claude Opus 4.7 system card](https://cdn.sanity.io/files/4zrzovbb/website/037f06850df7fbe871e206dad004c3db5fd50340.pdf)): What it evaluates: proof-based olympiad mathematics from the 2026 USA Mathematical Olympiad. Core idea: use recent proof-writing contest problems as a high-difficulty, lower-contamination complement to short-answer AIME-style tasks; this is a model-card evaluation reference rather than a standalone public benchmark package in this repository.
- [BeyondAIME](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ByteDance-Seed/BeyondAIME): What it evaluates: a supplementary competition-math set above AIME difficulty; core idea: separate model performance when `AIME` nears saturation, emphasizing higher difficulty and deeper reasoning.
- [MathArenaApex](https://matharena.ai/apex/) ([dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/MathArena/apex_2025)): What it evaluates: a set of "harder problems" selected from multiple competitions; core idea: use hard-problem aggregation for more stable discrimination, making it convenient for test-time scaling or multi-sampling comparisons.
- [MathArenaApex (shortlist)](https://huggingface.co/datasets/MathArena/apex-shortlist): What it evaluates: a smaller and harder subset of difficult problems; core idea: useful for fast evaluation iteration or comparison under high-cost reasoning settings.
- [GSM8K](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14168) ([code](https://github.com/openai/grade-school-math)): What it evaluates: text-based mathematical reasoning over multi-step grade-school word problems; core idea: use frequent, reproducible elementary problem types to characterize basic arithmetic and multi-step reasoning chains, making it a common starting point for informal mathematics.
- [ProofNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12433) ([code](https://github.com/zhangir-azerbayev/ProofNet)): What it evaluates: formal proof for undergraduate mathematics problems; core idea: cover more proof patterns through systematic course-level problems, often used in combined "drafting to formalization" evaluation.
- [TheoremQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12524) ([code](https://github.com/wenhuchen/TheoremQA)): What it evaluates: theorem-driven mathematics and science QA; core idea: require models to identify applicable theorems and complete symbolic or numerical reasoning, making it an important bridge from word problems to theorem-grounded reasoning.
- [MathVista](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.02255) ([code](https://github.com/lupantech/MathVista)): What it evaluates: mathematical reasoning over charts, geometry, and visual problem statements; core idea: integrate visual-textual information into the mathematical solving chain, emphasizing coupling between visual parsing and symbolic reasoning.
- [OlympiadBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14008) ([code](https://github.com/OpenBMB/OlympiadBench)): What it evaluates: competition-level mathematics and physics problems, including bilingual Chinese/English problems, diagram problems, and proof problems; core idea: complex reasoning closer to olympiad scenarios, covering multilingual and multimodal input, suitable as a more realistic and harder comprehensive stress test.
- [MathVision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14804): What it evaluates: visual mathematics problems, with more emphasis on mathematical information in diagrams, tables, or visual presentation; core idea: drive mathematical reasoning with visual input, often requiring OCR, structured parsing, and executable computation to work together.
- [MathVerse](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14624) ([code](https://github.com/ZrrSkywalker/MathVerse)): What it evaluates: visual mathematics combining charts, geometry diagrams, and problem text; core idea: use contrasts among text, image, and text-removed settings to disentangle "understanding the figure" from "solving the problem."
- [LiveBench Mathematics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19314) ([code](https://github.com/LiveBench/LiveBench)): What it evaluates: the mathematics component of a dynamically updated comprehensive leaderboard. Core idea: reduce contamination in static mathematics sets through continuously refreshed questions and compare mathematical capability within a unified general model evaluation framework.
- [PutnamBench / Putnam-200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11214) ([code](https://github.com/trishullab/PutnamBench)): What it evaluates: formal proof and verifiable solving for Putnam competition problems; core idea: explicitly define `Lean + Python + search tools` as a multi-turn agent runtime, making the benchmark itself closer to a benchmark-side harness definition with proof assistant in the loop.
- [GSM-Symbolic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05229) ([code](https://github.com/apple/ml-gsm-symbolic)): What it evaluates: symbolic variable substitution and difficulty perturbation for GSM8K-style problems; core idea: use controllable variants to test whether models rely on surface patterns rather than robust mathematical reasoning.
- [Omni-MATH](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07985) ([project page](https://omni-math.github.io/)): What it evaluates: olympiad-level mathematics problems with finer-grained domain and difficulty stratification; core idea: replace traditional sets that are becoming saturated, emphasizing subdomain diagnosis and interpretable difficulty levels.
- [DynaMath](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00836): What it evaluates: more dynamic and diverse visual mathematical reasoning; core idea: reduce template-based answering and emphasize robust generalization through richer changes and combinations in problem presentation.
- [FrontierMath / Epoch Benchmarking Hub](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04872) ([official benchmark page](https://epoch.ai/benchmarks/frontiermath)): What it evaluates: expert-written frontier problems with automatic verification; core idea: strongly emphasize anti-contamination and verifiable interfaces, using a small number of extremely hard and checkable problems to characterize the reasoning ceiling.
- [HARP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.08819): What it evaluates: anti-contamination evaluation with stronger emphasis on difficult mathematical reasoning. Core idea: reduce the impact of memorized problem hits through stricter data recency and problem composition.
- [The Karp Dataset](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14705): What it evaluates: mathematical reasoning over detailed proofs of NP-completeness reductions. Core idea: move beyond numeric word problems into theoretical-computer-science proof construction, with reductions ranging from undergraduate exercises to reductions from research papers.
- [MathConstruct](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10197): What it evaluates: constructive proofs and constructive mathematical problems; core idea: use "give an object/construction satisfying the conditions" as the main pressure point, complementing mathematical evaluations that only require final numeric or multiple-choice answers.
- [NumericBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11075): What it evaluates: fundamental numerical abilities such as number recognition, arithmetic, magnitude comparison, retrieval, summarization, and numerical logic. Core idea: expose low-level numeracy failures that can be hidden by high scores on structured mathematics benchmarks.
- [OlymMATH](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21380): What it evaluates: olympiad-style high-difficulty mathematical tasks. Core idea: characterize the upper bound of model capability in hard mathematical reasoning with problems closer to real competition difficulty distributions.
- [PolyMath](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18428) ([code](https://github.com/QwenLM/PolyMath), [dataset](https://hf.co/datasets/Qwen/PolyMath)): What it evaluates: mathematical reasoning in 18 languages and four difficulty levels. Core idea: keep mathematics as the main task axis while explicitly measuring multilingual transfer, language-consistency failures, and difficulty-sensitive degradation.
- [ProverBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21801) ([code](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-Prover-V2)): What it evaluates: Lean 4 formal mathematical proof, including 325 formalized problems and some 2024-2025 AIME problems; core idea: supplement miniF2F/PutnamBench with a verifiable formal-proving stress test, observing the gap between natural-language reasoning and formal proof.
- [FormalMATH](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.02735): What it evaluates: formal mathematical reasoning capability of large models; core idea: move natural-language math problems into proof-assistant-verifiable environments, focusing on formal statement, proof search, and verification loops.
- [DeepMath-Creative](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.08744): What it evaluates: mathematical creativity in large language models. Core idea: test whether models can produce non-routine constructions and creative solution ideas rather than only solve standard mathematical questions.
- [HARDMath2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11774) ([code](https://github.com/JamesRoggeveen/hardmath2_eval), [dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/JVRoggeveen/HARDMath2)): What it evaluates: applied mathematics problems with approximation-heavy scientific and engineering methods. Core idea: use graduate-class topics such as asymptotics, WKB methods, boundary layers, and nonlinear PDEs to cover problems where exact closed-form solutions are not the central skill.
- [MathArena Kangaroo (MathKangaroo)](https://matharena.ai/kangaroo/) ([dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/MathArena/kangaroo_2025)): What it evaluates: mathematics problems based on the Kangaroo competition, often in image-text or multiple-choice formats; core idea: use more popular and broader competition problem types to test robustness under a mixed easy/medium/hard distribution.
- [RealMath](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12575): What it evaluates: research-level mathematical tasks from math papers and math forums. Core idea: combine verifiable research questions, forum discussions, and continuous refresh mechanisms to reduce static competition-problem contamination and more directly test model understanding of real mathematical research materials.
- [MathArena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23281) ([code](https://github.com/eth-sri/matharena)): What it evaluates: anti-contamination evaluation and continuous updating over recent competition problems; core idea: incorporate "publication time" and "evaluation time" into the protocol, using problems closer to real release cadence to mitigate contamination.
- [VideoMathQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05349) ([project page](https://mbzuai-oryx.github.io/VideoMathQA); [code](https://github.com/mbzuai-oryx/VideoMathQA)): What it evaluates: multimodal mathematical reasoning in video. Core idea: organize lecture videos, visual problem statements, audio, and text clues into mathematical QA to test whether models can extract formulas, concepts, and reasoning steps from dynamic teaching/problem-solving processes.
- [ReliableMath](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.03133): What it evaluates: reliable mathematical reasoning over solvable and unsolvable cases. Core idea: test whether models can avoid fabricated answers when problem conditions are impossible or exceed their capability.
- [LLMThinkBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04023): What it evaluates: the accuracy-efficiency tradeoff in basic mathematical reasoning. Core idea: use dynamically generated basic math tasks and an overthinking score to diagnose when reasoning models spend excessive tokens without accuracy gains.
- [Putnam-AXIOM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.08292): What it evaluates: Putnam-style university competition mathematics and programmatically perturbed variants. Core idea: add contamination-robust variants beyond natural-language proof evaluation to check whether models truly master solution structure rather than reciting seen solutions.
- [Beyond Solving Math Quiz](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11252): What it evaluates: whether reasoning models ask for missing information before solving underspecified mathematics problems. Core idea: add incomplete-problem cases to math evaluation so proactivity, overthinking, and hallucinated assumptions are visible rather than scoring only final-answer accuracy.
- [MathCanvas](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.14958) ([project page](https://mathcanvas.github.io/)): What it evaluates: multimodal mathematics with stronger emphasis on sketches, diagrams, and "reasoning on a canvas"; core idea: bind mathematical reasoning with visual expression, requiring models to map back and forth between multimodal representations.
- [IMO-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.01846) ([project page](https://imobench.github.io/); [code](https://github.com/google-deepmind/superhuman/tree/main/imobench)): What it evaluates: a unified protocol for the IMO task family; core idea: split `short-answer verifiable` tasks (IMO-AnswerBench), `long-proof generation` (ProofBench), and `proof grading` (GradingBench) into reproducible sub-benchmarks and protocols, explicitly distinguishing answer solving from proof evaluation.
- [LiveMathBench](https://huggingface.co/datasets/opencompass/LiveMathBench): What it evaluates: recently and continuously updated mathematical evaluation. Core idea: use timely questions to reduce contamination and provide capability observations closer to the real deployment period.
- [MathHalu](https://doi.org/10.1109/icassp55912.2026.11460764): What it evaluates: hallucination detection in mathematical reasoning processes. Core idea: score intermediate solution steps so plausible-looking but unsupported mathematical chains are visible before final-answer grading.
- [FormalProofBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26996): What it evaluates: Lean 4 proof generation for advanced undergraduate and graduate mathematics. Core idea: pair natural-language problems with formal statements and require checker-accepted proofs, extending formal-proof evaluation beyond olympiad or undergraduate sets.
- [LiveMathematicianBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01754) ([project page](https://livemathematicianbench.github.io/); [dataset](https://huggingface.co/LiveMathematicianBench)): What it evaluates: theorem understanding and proof-sketch selection based on the latest arXiv papers; core idea: suppress contamination through continuously updated sources and distinguish surface matching from real mathematical understanding.
- [Beyond Accuracy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.06799): What it evaluates: algebraic reasoning failures under independently controlled complexity factors. Core idea: vary nesting, operator hardness, intermediate-state count, and dependency-chain structure to attribute failures beyond a single accuracy score.
- [MathNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18584) ([project page](https://mathnet.mit.edu/)): What it evaluates: multilingual, multimodal olympiad problems and mathematical retrieval; core idea: put `problem solving + mathematical semantic retrieval + RAG` into one protocol, specifically testing both "can solve problems" and "can find similar problems."
- [Beyond Benchmarks: MathArena as an Evaluation Platform](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00674): What it evaluates: extending MathArena from a one-off benchmark into a continuous mathematics evaluation platform; core idea: continuously add new competitions, new problem types, and unified protocols so the leaderboard rotates difficulty as model capabilities improve.

## 1.8.3 Agent Harness

- [Draft, Sketch, and Prove](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.12283) ([code](https://github.com/albertqjiang/draft_sketch_prove)): A two-stage formal proving harness that first generates proof sketches, then calls provers/hammers to complete formal proofs.
- [PAL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10435) ([code](https://github.com/reasoning-machines/pal)): Rewrites mathematical reasoning as executable programs and then fills in the answer, a classic starting point for programmatic math agents.
- [Program of Thoughts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12588) ([code](https://github.com/TIGER-AI-Lab/Program-of-Thoughts)): Explicitly converts intermediate reasoning into program execution traces, reducing arithmetic errors in pure-text reasoning.
- [Tree of Thoughts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10601) ([code](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/tree-of-thought-llm)): Tree-structures reasoning trajectories and supports branch expansion, backtracking, and self-evaluation; a representative harness for mathematical test-time search.
- [LeanDojo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15626) ([code](https://github.com/lean-dojo/LeanDojo)): Integrates premise retrieval, proof-state interaction, and verifier feedback into a reproducible proof-assistant-in-the-loop runtime.
- [ToRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17452) ([code](https://github.com/microsoft/ToRA)): Orchestrates natural-language reasoning, code execution, and symbolic tool calls into a unified math agent loop.
- [COPRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04353) ([code](https://github.com/trishullab/copra)): A collaborative proof agent for Lean, emphasizing planning, retrieval, and a proof-state-driven execution loop.
- [DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08152) ([code](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-Prover-V1.5)): A specialized agent implementation for Lean formal proof, emphasizing verifiable proof trajectories.
- [Newclid](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11938) ([code](https://github.com/Newclid/Newclid)): A neuro-symbolic agent framework for geometry proving, emphasizing a verifiable search process.
- [AlphaGeometry](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06747-5) ([code](https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphageometry)): A neuro-symbolic geometry proving system that constrains proof correctness with verifiable symbolic deduction.
- [AlphaGeometry 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03544) ([code](https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphageometry2)): A follow-up AlphaGeometry system that strengthens collaboration between geometry reasoning and symbolic search.
- [Goedel-Prover](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.07640) ([code](https://github.com/Goedel-LM/Goedel-Prover)): An agent system for formal mathematical proof, highlighting automated proof search and verifiable output.
- [StepMathAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10105): A process-evaluation agent for mathematical solutions; the core idea is to segment, score, aggregate, and organize step errors through a Tree-of-Error representation instead of only judging final answers.
- [DeepSeek-Prover-V2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21801) ([code](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-Prover-V2)): A recursive subgoal-decomposition formal proving harness for Lean 4; the core idea is to first have a strong model generate proof plans and subgoals, then use a prover to search for subgoal proofs and use Lean-verifiable feedback for cold start and reinforcement learning.
- [Prover Agent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19923) ([code](https://github.com/kAIto47802/Prover-Agent)): Improves Lean proof-solving capability through agentic search and tool interaction.
- [Seed-Prover](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.23726) ([code](https://github.com/ByteDance-Seed/Seed-Prover)): A lemma-style whole-proof proving system for IMO-level problems; the core idea is to iteratively revise proofs using Lean feedback, proven lemmas, and self-summarization, while combining depth and breadth search strategies at test time.
- [Goedel-Prover-V2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03613) ([code](https://github.com/Goedel-LM/Goedel-Prover-V2)): A self-correcting formal proof harness; the core idea is to improve proof-search stability and verification rate with difficulty-progressive data scaffolding, self-correction driven by Lean compiler feedback, and checkpoint averaging.
- [QED](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24021): A multi-agent proof-generation system for open mathematical problems; the core idea is to distribute proof search, critique, verification focus, and revision across specialized agents to address research-level proving failure modes.
- [OProver](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.17283): An agentic formal proving framework for Lean 4; the core idea is to iteratively feed failed proof attempts, compiler feedback, and verified proofs back into the training and repair loop.
- [STAR-PólyaMath](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19338) ([code](https://github.com/Julius-Woo/STAR-PolyaMath)): A multi-agent mathematical reasoning harness for leaderboards such as MathArena Apex, AIME, Putnam, IMO, HMMT, and USAMO; the core idea is to use a Meta-Strategist, Reasoner-Verifier, and challenge-step-replan state machine to maintain long-horizon strategies, backtrack failed branches, and limit hallucination accumulation.

## 1.8.4 Skill

- [sympy](https://github.com/davila7/claude-code-templates/tree/main/cli-tool/components/skills/scientific/sympy) is the most practical executable math skill, suitable for the `PAL / PoT / ToRA` line.
- [math-reasoning](https://skills.sh/lingzhi227/agent-research-skills/math-reasoning) is suitable for organizing mathematical solutions into verifiable and LaTeX-ready structures.
- [lean4](https://github.com/cameronfreer/lean4-skills/tree/main/plugins/lean4/skills/lean4) is suitable for Lean 4 proof workflows.
- [lean4-theorem-proving](https://github.com/benchflow-ai/skillsbench/tree/main/tasks/lean4-proof/environment/skills/lean4-theorem-proving) is suitable for long-horizon formal proof tasks.
- [lean4-memories](https://github.com/benchflow-ai/skillsbench/tree/main/tasks/lean4-proof/environment/skills/lean4-memories) is suitable for accumulating failed proof trajectories and reusable strategic memory.
- [benchflow-ai/skillsbench/sympy](https://github.com/benchflow-ai/skillsbench/tree/main/tasks/crystallographic-wyckoff-position-analysis/environment/skills/sympy) (a symbolic mathematics skill covering algebra, calculus, matrices, number theory, and code generation) is also suitable as a supplementary executable tool layer.
