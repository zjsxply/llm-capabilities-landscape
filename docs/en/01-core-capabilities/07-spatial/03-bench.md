# 1.7.3 Bench

- `CountBench` (see [Teaching CLIP to Count to Ten](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12066)): What it evaluates: reliability of visual counting and quantity concepts; core idea: counting is often a foundational diagnostic for spatial and compositional reasoning, so it can serve as an auxiliary characterization dimension for spatial reasoning evaluation.
- [BLINK](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12390) ([code](https://github.com/zeyofu/BLINK_Benchmark)): What it evaluates: core visual perception and spatially related capabilities of multimodal models, such as relative depth, correspondence, and multi-view consistency; core idea: rewrite classic CV perception tasks as multiple-choice VQA, emphasizing the gap between "visible" and "actually perceived."
- `DA-2K` ([Depth Anything V2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09414)): What it evaluates: single-image depth, relative depth, and other visual capabilities strongly tied to 3D geometry; core idea: use relative depth and geometric consistency as foundational observation signals for spatial understanding, though the task is closer to traditional vision evaluation than conversational reasoning.
- [SPACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06468) ([code](https://github.com/apple/ml-space-benchmark)): What it evaluates: spatial cognition in frontier models, covering navigational large-scale space, object shape/layout, and spatial attention/memory; core idea: rewrite classic cognitive-science spatial tasks into paired text and visual presentation protocols to distinguish language memory, visuospatial representation, and embodied navigation.
- [3DSRBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07825) ([project page](https://3dsrbench.github.io/)): What it evaluates: comprehensive 3D spatial reasoning capability; core idea: organize 3D reasoning needs such as distance, direction, occlusion, and viewpoint transformation into systematic question types, making it a strong baseline in the SPAR-Bench citation chain.
- [VSI-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.14171) ([code](https://github.com/vision-x-nyu/thinking-in-space)): What it evaluates: visual-spatial memory and recall in real spaces; core idea: use scene observation, memory retention, and spatial QA to test whether an MLLM can form stable spatial representations rather than only recognizing single-frame content.
- [MapEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00316) ([code](https://github.com/MapEval/MapEval-API)): What it evaluates: map/geospatial reasoning and route, neighborhood, and place-information retrieval; core idea: put map tools, heterogeneous geographic context, and compositional reasoning into the same evaluation protocol.
- [RefDrone](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00392): Evaluates referring expression comprehension in drone scenes. Core idea: use aerial-view targets and relation-heavy language to test whether models can ground references under scale, viewpoint, and spatial-relation ambiguity.
- [Hypo3D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00954): What it evaluates: 3D hypothetical reasoning; core idea: ask models counterfactual spatial questions such as what would happen if an object, viewpoint, or state changed, complementing static relation recognition with imaginative 3D reasoning.
- [ERQA](https://github.com/embodiedreasoning/ERQA): What it evaluates: embodied/physical-environment reasoning QA, including spatial relations, actions, and physical commonsense; core idea: place "spatial understanding" in an embodied context and test physical and spatial inference through QA closer to robotics scenarios.
- [SPAR-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22976) ([code](https://github.com/fudan-zvg/SPAR)): What it evaluates: 3D spatial perception and reasoning in VLMs, covering single-view and multi-view inputs; core idea: generate multi-level spatial QA from scenes with 3D ground truth, covering depth, distance, relations, and imaginative spatial reasoning.
- [Compositional-ARC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01445): What it evaluates: systematic generalization in abstract spatial reasoning. Core idea: test whether models can compose known geometric transformations, such as translation and rotation, into unseen combinations rather than memorizing individual ARC-style patterns.
- [PhysBench](https://physbench.github.io/#leaderboard): What it evaluates: physical-world understanding and spatial/causal reasoning. Core idea: put spatial relations, object motion, stability, and interaction-outcome prediction into a unified leaderboard protocol for screening spatial reasoning models with physical grounding.
- [AerialVG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07836): Evaluates visual grounding from aerial views. Core idea: use high-resolution aerial images and relation-heavy descriptions to test whether models can ground targets by positional relations when appearance cues are insufficient.
- [All-Angles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15280) ([code](https://github.com/Chenyu-Wang567/All-Angles-Bench)): What it evaluates: multi-view understanding, including egocentric and exocentric views, and cross-view consistent reasoning; core idea: alignment and reasoning across different views of the same scene are key difficulties in spatial intelligence, and the benchmark exposes this explicitly through multi-view QA.
- [PointArena (PointBench)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09990) ([code](https://github.com/pointarena/pointarena)): What it evaluates: pointing ability, requiring models to click/select or localize targets or regions in an image; core idea: move spatial grounding from text description to actionable pointing output, making localization error measurable.
- [ViewSpatial-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21500) ([code](https://github.com/ZJU-REAL/ViewSpatial-Bench)): What it evaluates: multi-view spatial localization and viewpoint transformation; core idea: jointly examine camera-centered and human-centered spatial frames, exposing the performance gap when models generalize from egocentric to allocentric reasoning.
- [Ego3D-Bench](https://vbdi.github.io/Ego3D-Bench-webpage/#leaderboard): What it evaluates: egocentric 3D spatial understanding. Core idea: use depth, direction, viewpoint transformation, and spatial-memory tasks in egocentric observations to test whether models can form stable 3D representations from self-centered visual streams.
- [VLM4D](https://vlm4d.github.io/#leaderboard): What it evaluates: 4D spatiotemporal understanding. Core idea: bring dynamic 3D scenes, object motion, and cross-time spatial relations into a unified evaluation, complementing the limited temporal coverage of static-image spatial-relation benchmarks.
- [EASI Leaderboard Data](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lmms-lab-si/EASI-Leaderboard-Data/tree/main): What it evaluates: aggregated leaderboard data for spatial VLM benchmarks. Core idea: put spatial reasoning results from SPAR-Bench, MMSI-Bench, OmniSpatial, ViewSpatial, VSI-Bench, and related datasets into a unified data entry point for cross-benchmark comparison of model spatial intelligence.
- [MMSI-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23764) ([code](https://github.com/OpenRobotLab/MMSI-Bench)): What it evaluates: spatial intelligence under multi-image input, requiring models to form spatially consistent understanding across images; core idea: explicitly test cross-image fusion and spatial-relation reasoning with "multi-view/multi-frame" input rather than single-image QA.
- [CoSpace](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Zhu_CoSpace_Benchmarking_Continuous_Space_Perception_Ability_for_Vision-Language_Models_CVPR_2025_paper.html): Evaluates continuous space perception for VLMs. Core idea: move beyond discrete left/right or near/far labels and test whether models can reason over continuous spatial layouts.
- [DepthCues](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Danier_DepthCues_Evaluating_Monocular_Depth_Perception_in_Large_Vision_Models_CVPR_2025_paper.html): Evaluates monocular depth perception in large vision models. Core idea: test whether models understand depth cues from one image, a foundational spatial perception skill behind 3D reasoning.
- [Spatial457](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/html/Wang_Spatial457_A_Diagnostic_Benchmark_for_6D_Spatial_Reasoning_of_Large_CVPR_2025_paper.html): Evaluates 6D spatial reasoning in large multimodal models. Core idea: diagnose position, rotation, and viewpoint-related failures that are hidden by simpler 2D spatial relation tests.
- [OmniSpatial](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.03135): What it evaluates: comprehensive spatial reasoning, covering dynamic reasoning, complex spatial logic, spatial interaction, and perspective-taking; core idea: organize 50 fine-grained subcategories along cognitive-psychology capability axes, avoiding evaluation limited to low-level relations such as left/right and near/far.
- [RefSpatial-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04308) ([code](https://github.com/Zhoues/RoboRefer)): What it evaluates: spatial referring and spatial-relation understanding, often for robotics/embodied scenarios; core idea: define spatial referring as a reproducible runtime and put stronger emphasis on precise grounding of 3D/relative-position semantics.
- [MARBLE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.22992): What it evaluates: multimodal spatial reasoning and planning; core idea: use the M-Portal and M-Cube task types to examine how models construct and understand multi-step plans under visual, physical, and spatial constraints.
- [IR3D-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23329) ([project page](https://ir3d-bench.github.io/); [code](https://github.com/LiuHengyu321/IR3D-Bench); [dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Piang/IR3D-Bench)): What it evaluates: 3D scene graph generation from incomplete 3D reconstruction. Core idea: put missing data, noise, and relation inference in real 3D reconstruction into a single protocol, testing whether models can recover scene relations from imperfect spatial evidence.
- [PAC Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23725) ([project page](https://pacbench.github.io/); [dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lens-lab/pacbench)): What it evaluates: whether foundation models understand the preconditions of robot manipulation policies. Core idea: judge whether a manipulation policy is executable around object properties, affordances, and physical constraints, filling the gap in spatial/embodied tasks where models can see objects but do not know what can be done.
- [SpatialViz-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07610): Evaluates spatial visualization in MLLMs; core idea: ground diagnostic tasks in cognitive spatial-visualization constructs to separate mental rotation, transformation, and relation failures.
- [TreeBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07999) ([code](https://github.com/Haochen-Wang409/TreeVGR)): What it evaluates: traceable evidence chains and spatial/relation reasoning reliability in visually grounded reasoning; core idea: further constrain "correct answers" to "being able to point to evidence," binding reasoning more strongly to verifiable visual evidence organization.
- [B4DL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05269): Evaluates 4D LiDAR understanding for multimodal LLMs. Core idea: use modality-specific annotations and dynamic point-cloud tasks so models are tested on spatial geometry and temporal interactions rather than only image or video inputs.
- [MapIQ](https://openreview.net/forum?id=VSwRuGtB5n): Evaluates map question answering by multimodal LLMs. Core idea: test geospatial interpretation, map semantics, and route-style reasoning under a visual map interface.
- [VideoGameBench](https://vgbench.com/#leaderboard): What it evaluates: embodied perception, navigation, manipulation, and planning in video-game environments. Core idea: place spatial understanding in actionable game environments and use task completion and trajectory quality to evaluate whether agents can translate visual spatial relations into continuous actions.
- [STI-Bench](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/html/Li_STI-Bench_Are_MLLMs_Ready_for_Precise_Spatial-Temporal_World_Understanding_ICCV_2025_paper.html): Evaluates precise spatial-temporal world understanding in MLLMs. Core idea: bridge static spatial reasoning and dynamic video reasoning by asking for exact relations across space and time.
- [DSI-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18873): What it evaluates: dynamic 3D spatial intelligence; core idea: use nearly one thousand dynamic videos and nine observer/object motion patterns to distinguish ego-motion, object motion, and relative spatial-relation reasoning.
- [SpatialTree](https://spatialtree.github.io/#leaderboard): What it evaluates: tree-structured spatial reasoning. Core idea: require models to decompose complex spatial relations into hierarchical, traceable structures, observing whether spatial reasoning can compose from local relations to global judgments.
- [DecompSR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.02627): What it evaluates: decomposed compositional multi-hop spatial reasoning. Core idea: procedurally generate provably checked tasks while independently varying productivity, substitutivity, overgeneralization, and systematicity to diagnose where spatial generalization fails.
- [SpatialBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21471): What it evaluates: hierarchical spatial cognition in MLLMs; core idea: decompose spatial intelligence into a five-level framework from basic observation to high-level planning, and use 15 task types and capability-oriented metrics to measure whether models only perceive surfaces while lacking symbolic, causal, and planning abilities.
- [CartoMapQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03558) ([code](https://github.com/ungquanghuy-kddi/CartoMapQA)): What it evaluates: cartographic map understanding and geospatial QA; core idea: use tasks such as symbol recognition, embedded information extraction, scale interpretation, and route reasoning to expose VLM weaknesses in map semantics, OCR, and geospatial reasoning.
- [MMSI-Video-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10863) ([code](https://github.com/InternRobotics/MMSI-Video-Bench)): What it evaluates: spatial intelligence under video input; core idea: use 1,106 expert-annotated questions covering perception, planning, prediction, and cross-video reasoning to test whether models can maintain spatial layout, motion, and viewpoint consistency in continuous visual streams.
- [Grid Spatial Understanding (GSU)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17333): What it evaluates: text-only spatial reasoning over grids, embodied reference frames, and coordinate structures. Core idea: isolate spatial reasoning from visual perception through navigation, object-localization, and structure-composition tasks.
- [See, Remember, Explore / S3-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23864): What it evaluates: streaming spatial question answering with active exploration. Core idea: require agents to answer using only observations available up to a timestamp, optionally taking exploration actions to acquire missing evidence, so spatial reasoning is tested as an online memory and perception problem rather than post-hoc video QA.
- [Grid-world spatial world-model tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10690): Evaluates whether LLMs form robust spatial world models in maze tasks. Core idea: compare adjacency-token and visual-grid representations, sequential proximity questions, and compositional distance reasoning to expose representation-dependent spatial planning failures.
