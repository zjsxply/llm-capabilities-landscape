# 1.17.3 Bench

- [AI‐Powered Scoring for Creative Thinking: Methods and Challenges in PISA Assessment](https://doi.org/10.1002/jocb.70082): Reviews automated scoring routes for the PISA 2022 Creative Thinking assessment, contrasting semantic-distance methods, supervised fine-tuning, and few-shot or zero-shot LLM scoring.
- [Unpacking the Essential Tension of Knowledge Recombination: Analyzing the Impact of Knowledge Spanning on Citation Counts and Disruptive Innovation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09737): An analysis of American Physical Society papers operationalizes knowledge spanning and disruptive innovation, finding a U-shaped relationship between spanning and disruption but an inverted-U relationship with citations; it turns scientific recombination into measurable creativity outcomes.
- [Probing the "Creativity" of Large Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-emnlp.858/): Tests divergent semantic association with the Divergent Association Task. Core idea: ask models to generate unrelated words and score semantic distance, showing that decoding strategy and temperature can raise DAT scores while trading off stability.
- [Increasing Diversity While Maintaining Accuracy](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.34/): Evaluates human intervention for diverse LLM text-data generation. Core idea: combine logit suppression, temperature sampling, label replacement, and out-of-scope filtering to show how humans can recover accuracy lost when generation is pushed toward diversity.
- [The Crowdless Future? Generative AI and Creative Problem-Solving](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2023.18430): Benchmarks human crowd versus human-AI creative problem solving with differentiated and independent solution-search protocols.
- [Would Archimedes Shout “Eureka” with Algorithms? The Hidden Hand of Algorithmic Design in Idea Generation, the Creation of Ideation Bubbles, and How Experts Can Burst Them](https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2023.1307): Studies algorithmic design in idea generation, measuring ideation bubbles and expert interventions that can reopen narrowed creative search spaces.
- [Can AI Be as Creative as Humans?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01623) ([project page](https://ai-relative-creativity.github.io/)): Frames AI creativity as a measurable comparison against human creator groups. Core idea: define Relative Creativity and Statistical Creativity, then connect the theory to prompt-conditioned autoregressive models so creative ability can be assessed through conditional data fitting rather than a single universal creativity definition.
- [Can AI Write Classical Chinese Poetry like Humans? An Empirical Study Inspired by Turing Test](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04952): ProFTAP evaluates LLM classical Chinese poetry by mixing human and model poems, collecting probabilistic human-authorship judgments, and using AUC plus signed-rank tests to assess indistinguishability.
- [AesBench: An Expert Benchmark for Multimodal Large Language Models on Image Aesthetics Perception](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.08276) ([leaderboard](https://aesbench.github.io/)): Evaluates image-aesthetic perception in multimodal large language models. Core idea: use expert-designed aesthetic judgments to test whether MLLMs can recognize composition, color, style, and visual quality signals that matter when creative artifacts are evaluated.
- [Assessing and Understanding Creativity in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12491): Evaluates LLM creativity with a modified Torrance-style protocol. Core idea: use 700 questions across 7 tasks and four criteria, Fluency, Flexibility, Originality, and Elaboration, to show that LLMs are strong on elaboration but weaker on originality, with prompts, role play, and multi-LLM collaboration changing the profile.
- [Exploring Precision and Recall to assess the quality and diversity of LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10693): Imports precision and recall from image generation to open-ended text evaluation. Core idea: compare model outputs as distributions so quality and diversity can be measured without aligned references, exposing trade-offs hidden by ordinary benchmark accuracy.
- [An Interpretable Evaluation of Entropy-based Novelty of Generative Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17287): Proposes an interpretable entropy-based novelty evaluation for generative models, giving open-ended generation a distributional creativity metric.
- [The Effects of Generative AI on Design Fixation and Divergent Thinking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11164): Measures how generative AI assistance changes design fixation and divergent thinking. Core idea: compare human design behavior with and without AI-generated examples to test whether ideation support broadens the solution space or anchors users to early suggestions.
- [CreativEval: Evaluating Creativity of LLM-Based Hardware Code Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.08806): Evaluates creativity in RTL hardware-code generation. Core idea: score LLM-generated hardware designs along fluency, flexibility, originality, and elaboration so creative engineering output can be assessed beyond compilation success.
- [Characterising the Creative Process in Humans and Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00899): Measures creative process trajectories rather than only final scores. Core idea: use sentence embeddings to compare semantic-space exploration and jump profiles for humans and LLMs on Alternate Uses and Verbal Fluency tasks.
- [Divergent creativity in humans and large language models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13012): Compares LLMs with human divergent thinking at scale. Core idea: use computational-creativity metrics over 100,000 human responses to show that LLMs can exceed average humans on the Divergent Association Task while still trailing highly creative people.
- [Do Language Models Enjoy Their Own Stories?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13769): Evaluates whether LLMs can substitute for human annotators in automatic story evaluation. Core idea: compare LLM ratings, automatic metrics, and human annotations, showing stronger system-level story scoring than older automatic measures but weaker explanatory reliability.
- [A Robot Walks into a Bar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20956): Evaluates LLMs as comedy creativity-support tools with professional comedians. Core idea: combine workshops, the Creativity Support Index, and focus groups to show that current models often produce bland, biased, or over-filtered comedy rather than artist-aligned material.
- [Creativity Has Left the Chat: The Price of Debiasing Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05587): Measures the price of debiasing on LLM creativity, testing whether safety-oriented debiasing reduces originality or diversity in open-ended generation.
- [Humor in AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10522): Builds a large-scale cartoon-caption humor benchmark. Core idea: release more than 250 million crowd ratings over 2.2 million New Yorker caption-contest captions and use ranking evaluations to show that GPT-4 and Claude still lag top human contestants.
- [Evaluating n-Gram Novelty of Language Models Using Rusty-DAWG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13069): Measures how much generated text reuses training-corpus n-grams. Core idea: use a DAWG-style index for constant-time arbitrary-length n-gram lookup, revealing that LMs are less novel than human text for longer n-grams and that decoding choices shape novelty.
- [Are Large Language Models Capable of Generating Human-Level Narratives?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13248): Compares LLM and human narrative generation quality. Core idea: treat narrative writing as a creative capability test and analyze where model stories match or diverge from human-level coherence, interest, and storytelling quality.
- [Overview of the NLPCC 2024 Shared Task on Chinese Metaphor Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04378): Summarizes a Chinese metaphor-generation shared task. Core idea: make metaphor production a benchmarked creative-language task with a common dataset, submitted systems, and evaluation protocol for figurative generation.
- [The Double-Edged Roles of Generative AI in the Creative Process: Experiments on Design Work](https://doi.org/10.1287/isre.2024.0937): Uses lab studies and professional-designer tests to separate GenAI's ideation-stage gains from later design-stage limits in creative work.
- [Augmented Learning for Joint Creativity in Human-GenAI Co-Creation](https://doi.org/10.1287/isre.2024.0984): Tests whether human-GenAI co-creation produces augmented learning over repeated collaboration rather than only one-off creative output gains.
- [The Creative Psychometric Item Generator: a Framework for Item Generation and Validation Using Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.00202): The Creative Psychometric Item Generator uses LLMs to generate and validate creativity-test items, making psychometric creativity assessment itself a reproducible LLM-supported evaluation workflow.
- [Can Large Language Models Unlock Novel Scientific Research Ideas?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.06185): Evaluates research-idea generation from scientific papers. Core idea: target the hard-to-scale evaluation problem for future research ideas, where domain expertise and awareness of current literature make ordinary reference-based metrics inadequate.
- [SimulBench: Evaluating Language Models with Creative Simulation Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07641): SimulBench evaluates creative simulation tasks by using a fixed user agent to collect multi-turn scripts, then scoring target LLM final responses with GPT-4.
- [Evaluating and Enhancing Large Language Models for Novelty Assessment in Scholarly Publications](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16605): Introduces SchNovel for scholarly novelty assessment. Core idea: compare paper pairs across six arXiv fields by publication timing and add a retrieval-augmented novelty assessor that mimics reviewers checking similar prior work.
- [Human Creativity in the Age of LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03703): Runs randomized experiments on divergent and convergent thinking to measure how LLM assistance changes human creative performance rather than only model output quality.
- [AI as Humanity's Salieri](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04265): Quantifies linguistic creativity by attributing machine text against web text. Core idea: compare model generations with human-written web passages to test whether LLMs recombine common language patterns rather than matching expert human originality.
- [Towards Better Open-Ended Text Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.18653): Provides a multicriteria framework for open-ended text generation. Core idea: rank decoding methods with partial orders and summary metrics that balance coherence, diversity, and perplexity instead of optimizing a single automatic score.
- [Evaluating Creative Short Story Generation in Humans and Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02316): Benchmarks creative short-story writing across 60 LLMs and 60 people. Core idea: use cue-word five-sentence stories, automatic novelty and surprise metrics, and expert plus non-expert ratings to separate stylistic complexity from genuine originality.
- [Conditional Vendi Score](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02817): Measures diversity in prompt-conditioned generative models. Core idea: decompose generated-sample entropy into prompt-induced and model-induced diversity so image and video generators are not rewarded merely for varied prompts.
- [One fish, two fish, but not the whole sea](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04427): Evaluates conceptual diversity in synthetic LLM populations. Core idea: compare within-population and population-level variability before and after alignment, showing that alignment can reduce conceptual diversity relative to human behavioral data.
- [Metaphor generation based on noval evaluation method](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neucom.2024.128651): Releases MGPP, an 18,000-pair manually annotated metaphor-generation corpus, and MS-score, a metaphoricity-plus-similarity metric used to evaluate and rerank generated metaphors.
- [Human perception of art in the age of artificial intelligence](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1497469): Compares paired human-made and DALL·E 2 artworks with preference-choice and origin-discrimination tasks, measuring both aesthetic preference and detectability of AI-generated art.
- [One Does Not Simply Meme Alone: Evaluating Co-Creativity Between LLMs and Humans in the Generation of Humor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11433): Evaluates human-LLM co-creativity for meme and humor generation, focusing on whether collaboration improves joke quality, originality, and user creative control.
- [A Causality-Aware Paradigm for Evaluating Creativity of Multimodal Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15147): Proposes a causality-aware evaluation paradigm for multimodal LLM creativity, separating causal understanding from surface-level image-text novelty.
- [Why Do We Laugh? Annotation and Taxonomy Generation for Laughable Contexts in Spontaneous Text Conversation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16635): Annotates laughable contexts in Japanese spontaneous text conversations and uses LLM-generated explanations to build a ten-category taxonomy of why dialogue becomes laughable.
- [We're Different, We're the Same: Creative Homogeneity Across LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19361): Creative Homogeneity Across LLMs elicits creative responses from humans and multiple LLMs on standardized creativity tests, measuring population-level diversity to show stronger similarity among LLM outputs than among human outputs.
- [How do Humans and Language Models Reason About Creativity? A Comparative Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03253): Compares human and language-model creativity reasoning under a shared evaluation protocol, separating creative judgments from ordinary answer fluency.
- [Pencils to Pixels: A Systematic Study of Creative Drawings across Children, Adults and AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05999): Benchmarks visual creativity with 1,338 child, adult, and AI drawings, style/content metrics, and expert versus automated ratings.
- [Timing Matters: How Using LLMs at Different Timings Influences Writers' Perceptions and Ideation Outcomes in AI-Assisted Ideation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06197): Timing Matters evaluates when writers should consult LLMs during ideation, showing that the timing of assistance changes both perceived agency and the quality or diversity of resulting ideas.
- [Components of Creativity: Language Model-based Predictors for Clustering and Switching in Verbal Fluency](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.conll-1.15): Uses language-model predictors to estimate clustering and switching in verbal-fluency tasks, making components of divergent creativity measurable from generated word sequences.
- [Artistic turing test: The challenge of differentiating human and AI-generated art](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbah.2025.100209): Artistic Turing Test prompts AI systems from children's drawings and asks human and AI participants to classify human versus AI artworks, probing visual-art indistinguishability.
- [A Critical Assessment of Modern Generative Models' Ability to Replicate Artistic Styles](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15856): Evaluates artistic-style replication in modern generative models, turning style imitation into a concrete creativity test rather than judging sample appeal alone.
- [Integrating Generative Artificial Intelligence and Human Design: The Impact of Automation Level on Human Creative Experience and Efficiency](https://doi.org/10.1080/10447318.2025.2518333): Compares automation levels in generative-AI design support to measure effects on creative experience and efficiency.
- [Language Models and the Magic of Metaphor: A Comparative Evaluation with Human Judgments](https://aclanthology.org/2025.clicit-1.49/): Compares language-model metaphor interpretations with human judgments, turning figurative-language understanding into a creativity-adjacent evaluation protocol.
- [The Dynamics of Collective Creativity in Human-AI Hybrid Societies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17962): Models human-AI hybrid social networks to measure nonlinear effects of AI agents on idea spread, cultural artifacts, and collective creative dynamics.
- [Bridging the Creativity Understanding Gap: Small-Scale Human Alignment Enables Expert-Level Humor Ranking in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20356): Shows that small-scale human alignment can improve LLM humor ranking to expert-level judgments, making joke evaluation itself a measurable creative subskill.
- [Automated Evaluation of Meter and Rhyme in Russian Generative and Human-Authored Poetry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20931): Releases RIFMA and a Russian poetry scansion tool for stress placement, rhyme detection, and poeticness-defect identification, enabling LLM evaluation on formal verse constraints.
- [Figurative Archive: an open dataset and web-based application for the study of metaphor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00444): Provides 996 Italian metaphors with familiarity, semantic-distance, interpretation, and corpus-derived ratings plus a web application, making metaphor comprehension and figurative-language creativity easier to evaluate beyond ad hoc prompts.
- [Creation-Mmbench: Assessing Context-Aware Creative Intelligence in Mllms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14478): Creation-MMBench evaluates context-aware creative intelligence in MLLMs, testing whether models can generate and judge creative multimodal responses under contextual constraints.
- [AI Delivers Creative Output but Struggles with Thinking Processes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.23327): This study tests GPT-3.5, GPT-4, and GPT-4o against humans on divergent thinking, convergent thinking, insight problems, creative writing, association flow, representational change, and creative selection.
- [NoveltyBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05228) ([leaderboard](https://novelty-bench.github.io/)): Evaluates whether LMs can generate multiple distinct high-quality responses. Core idea: curate prompts and real user queries that invite many valid answers, then show that 20 leading models generate less distributional diversity than human writers and that larger models can be less diverse within a family.
- [Measuring LLM Novelty As The Frontier Of Original And High-Quality Output](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09389): Measuring LLM Novelty treats novelty as the frontier where outputs are both original and high quality, giving creativity evaluation a metric that does not reward random divergence alone.
- [Has the Creativity of Large-Language Models peaked?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12320): Re-evaluates inter- and intra-model variability on creativity tasks, showing whether recent LLM gains saturate under repeated creative-assessment probes.
- [Probing and Inducing Combinational Creativity in Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13120): CreativeMashup evaluates VLM combinational creativity with 666 artist-made visual mashups annotated by the IEI levels of identification, explanation, and implication.
- [Roll the dice & look before you leap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15266) ([open-source code](https://github.com/chenwu98/algorithmic-creativity)): Provides a cross-cutting test-bed for algorithmic creativity beyond next-token prediction. Core idea: quantify coherence, diversity, and originality on controllable tasks that require a far-sighted stochastic plan, making creative generation measurable without relying on subjective real-world judging.
- [Automated Creativity Evaluation for Large Language Models: A Reference-Based Approach](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15784): Introduces a reference-based automated evaluator for LLM creativity, giving creative-output scoring a more reproducible alternative to ad hoc judge prompts.
- [Homogenizing effect of large language models (LLMs) on creative diversity: An empirical comparison of human and ChatGPT writing](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbah.2025.100207): Compares human writing with ChatGPT writing to measure whether LLM assistance narrows creative diversity and homogenizes open-ended texts.
- [Artificial Intelligence and the Creative Process: Does AI-Creativity Extend Beyond Divergent Thinking?](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yjoc.2025.100105): Tests whether AI creativity extends beyond divergent-thinking measures, pushing evaluation toward broader stages of the creative process.
- [Exploring Cognitive Strategies in Human-AI Interaction: ChatGPT's Role in Creative Tasks](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yjoc.2025.100095): Studies ChatGPT's role in creative tasks through the cognitive strategies people use while interacting with the model.
- [Unravelling technostress in human–AI collaborative brainstorming: examining the impact of AI technology characteristics on brainstorming outcomes through AI control anxiety](https://doi.org/10.1108/k-11-2025-2887): Studies human-AI collaborative brainstorming with 409 participants, linking AI technology characteristics and AI-control anxiety to creative performance during idea generation and selection preference during idea selection.
- [A Principle-Based Evaluation of Generative AI Animation](https://doi.org/10.1109/tensymp63728.2025.11145008): Evaluates five AI-generated animations against ten classical animation principles using expert ratings, inter-rater reliability, chi-square tests, factor analysis, ANOVA, and logistic regression.
- [To Feel is to be Human? The Empirical Study of Reader Response to Original and AI-Generated Texts](https://doi.org/10.5709/dialogica-01.01.2025-04): Compares reader responses to Poe originals and LLM-generated variants using Likert and semantic-differential scales, making authorship framing and literary form part of the AI creative-writing evaluation protocol.
- [Evaluation of Generated Poetry](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.eval4nlp-1.9): Proposes automatic poetry metrics for rhyme, metre, syntax, semantics, and unknown-word usage, then validates them on Czech generated and human-written poems against human judgments.
- [Harmonious Minds: Benchmarking Intertwined Reasoning of Human Personality and Musical Preference](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.findings-ijcnlp.126): Benchmarks whether LLMs can infer personality traits from text and select chord-progressions that match musical preferences, testing cross-domain reasoning over psychological cues and symbolic music structure.
- [Optimising ChatGPT for creativity in literary translation: A case study from English into Dutch, Chinese, Catalan and Spanish](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18221): Evaluates ChatGPT literary-translation creativity across Dutch, Chinese, Catalan, and Spanish, varying prompts and generation settings to separate fidelity from creative departure.
- [Defining and Quantifying Creative Behavior in Popular Image Generators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04497): Introduces practical quantitative measures for image-generator creativity and checks them against human intuition, helping compare image-to-image models by creative behavior rather than sample appeal alone.
- [Creativity or Brute Force? Using Brainteasers as a Window into the Problem-Solving Abilities of Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10844): Introduces long-narrative brainteasers whose solutions can be either insight-based or brute-force, evaluating parsing, solving, self-correction, solution sketches, and hint use to expose when LLMs find creative shortcuts.
- [Can LLMs Simulate Human Behavioral Variability? A Case Study in the Phonemic Fluency Task](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16164): Compares 34 models and 45 configurations against 106 humans on the phonemic fluency task, using output diversity and lexical-network structure to test whether LLMs reproduce human creative variability.
- [Not All Jokes Land: Evaluating Large Language Models Understanding of Workplace Humor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01819): Evaluates LLM understanding of workplace humor, testing whether models can recognize joke intent, appropriateness, and contextual cues in professional communication.
- [The Reader is the Metric: How Textual Features and Reader Profiles Explain Conflicting Evaluations of AI Creative Writing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.03310): The Reader is the Metric models 101 annotators over 1,471 stories with 17 reference-less textual features, separating surface-focused and holistic creative-writing evaluators.
- [Can Artificial Intelligence Write Like Borges? An Evaluation Protocol for Spanish Microfiction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08172): Defines a literary-theory-grounded evaluation protocol for AI-generated Spanish microfiction, scoring thematic coherence, textual clarity, interpretive depth, and aesthetic quality against Borges-style short fiction expectations.
- [Can Recombination Displace Dominant Scientific Ideas](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15959): Using 41 million papers from 1965 to 2024, the study separates atypical cross-topic recombination from disruption within an established topic; the two measures capture different paths to scientific novelty and expose why more recombination need not produce more breakthroughs.
- [The Ideation-Execution Gap: Execution Outcomes of LLM-Generated versus Human Research Ideas](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20803): Compares LLM-generated and human research ideas by downstream execution outcomes, testing whether apparently creative scientific proposals survive implementation and evaluation.
- [MetaClimage: A novel database of visual metaphors related to Climate Change, with costs and benefits analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09225): Releases a climate-change visual-metaphor database paired with literal images and human ratings for difficulty, efficacy, artistic quality, emotional arousal, and tag-derived semantic and emotion variables, supporting multimodal metaphor evaluation beyond literal image description.
- [A Comparative Approach to Assessing Linguistic Creativity of Large Language Models and Humans](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12039): Compares LLM and human linguistic creativity under a shared assessment setup, helping separate model fluency from human-like originality.
- [Metaphor and Large Language Models: When Surface Features Matter More than Deep Understanding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15357): Benchmarks LLM metaphor understanding under surface-feature perturbations, showing when apparent figurative comprehension comes from shallow cues.
- [Which LLMs Get the Joke? Probing Non-STEM Reasoning Abilities with HumorBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21476): HumorBench probes which LLMs can get jokes, using humor as a non-STEM reasoning benchmark that stresses incongruity, cultural cues, and pragmatic interpretation.
- [Automating chemosensory creativity assessment with large language models](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodqual.2025.105599): Uses LLMs to automate assessment of chemosensory creativity, turning flavor and food-idea originality into a domain-specific scoring problem rather than relying only on manual expert ratings.
- [Human creativity versus artificial intelligence: source attribution, observer attitudes, and eye movements while viewing visual art](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1509974): Records eye movements while participants view visual art attributed to humans or AI, linking explicit source-attribution attitudes with implicit perceptual behavior.
- [On choosing the vehicles of metaphors without a body: evidence from Large Language Models](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.analogyangle-1.4): Tests large language models on metaphor-vehicle choice, using embodiment as the lens for whether figurative-language judgments match human metaphor reasoning.
- [CordelSextilha.BR: A Benchmark for Poetic Form in Brazilian Cordel Verse Generation](https://doi.org/10.5753/eniac.2025.14067): Provides a Brazilian cordel sextilha generation benchmark with 1,519 public-domain stanzas, final-line completion, expert ratings for rhyme, meter, coherence, and cordelisticity, plus RhythmAcc and RhymeAcc baselines for GPT-4o and LLaMA-3.2.
- [PoeTone: A Framework for Constrained Generation of Structured Chinese Songci with LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02515): Evaluates 18 LLMs on Chinese Songci generation with formal conformity, LLM quality judging, human evaluation, and probing tasks, then uses the evaluator as a critic in a Generate-Critic architecture.
- [The Basic B*** Effect: The Use of LLM-based Agents Reduces the Distinctiveness and Diversity of People's Choices](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02910): Measures how generic and personalized LLM agents change 110,000 real identity-relevant choices from 1,000 U.S. users, showing reduced interpersonal distinctiveness and intrapersonal diversity.
- [Language Model Perplexity Predicts Scientific Surprise and Transformative Impact](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05591): Across more than two million papers, language-model perplexity predicts reviewer disagreement, editorial delay, interdisciplinary engagement, and later transformative impact; the most surprising papers produce a bimodal mix of celebrated breakthroughs and rejected work, making scientific surprise testable.
- [D-HUMOR: Dark Humor Understanding via Multimodal Open-ended Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06771): Releases 4,379 Reddit memes annotated for dark-humor target category and intensity, then evaluates a role-reversal self-loop with tri-stream reasoning for multimodal open-ended humor understanding.
- [Pun Unintended: LLMs and the Illusion of Humor Understanding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12158): Pun Unintended evaluates whether LLMs genuinely understand puns and humor or merely produce plausible explanations, making humor understanding a diagnostic creativity benchmark rather than a surface fluency test.
- [Combinatorial Creativity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21043): Evaluates open-ended combinatorial creativity beyond compositional generalization. Core idea: define novelty-utility scoring over algorithmic creative tasks, then use it to diagnose creativity scaling, depth-width tradeoffs, and the ideation-execution gap where novel outputs become less useful.
- [Death of the Novel(ty): Beyond n-Gram Novelty as a Metric for Textual Creativity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22641): Uses 8,618 expert-writer annotations to show that n-gram novelty alone over-rewards nonsensical or impractical expressions, separating textual creativity from surface rarity.
- [The Geometry of Creative Variability: How Credal Sets Expose Calibration Gaps in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23088): Uses credal sets to measure how model story distributions cover human creative variation across prompts, models, and decoding strategies.
- [Galton's Law of Mediocrity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25767): Evaluates why LLM-generated advertising copy regresses toward average ideas, making originality failure visible in a creative domain with human comparison points.
- [UPMVM: A Metrics Verification Model for Urdu Poetry](https://doi.org/10.9781/ijimai.2025.09.001): Defines a rule-based Urdu ghazal meter detector and custom system for verifying Arud meters in poetry lines.
- [Style Over Story: A Process-Oriented Study of Authorial Creativity in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02025): Style Over Story studies authorial creativity in LLMs as a process, separating stylistic polish from deeper story development so creative-writing evaluation does not collapse into fluency scoring.
- [What Shapes a Creative Machine Mind? Comprehensively Benchmarking Creativity in Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04009): What Shapes a Creative Machine Mind benchmarks creativity in foundation models across multiple task and scoring axes, making model creativity a structured evaluation target rather than a single prompt demo.
- [Unveiling LLMs' Metaphorical Understanding: Exploring Conceptual Irrelevance, Context Leveraging and Syntactic Influence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04120): Evaluates LLM metaphor processing through concept mapping, metaphor-literal repositories, and syntactic-sensitivity tests, exposing when models use context versus irrelevant literal mappings.
- [How Well do LLMs Understand Sinhala Metaphors? Insights from SinMet-5K](https://doi.org/10.1109/scse70081.2026.11499936): Introduces SinMet-5K for Sinhala metaphor understanding, testing whether LLMs can interpret low-resource figurative language rather than only English-centric metaphors.
- [Analysis of Text Accuracy and Visual Alignment in Vision-Language Models for Artistic Text Generation](https://doi.org/10.1109/wacv61042.2026.00092): This WACV benchmark analyzes DALL-E, Qwen-VL, and Qwen-2.5B on artistic text generation with 1,000 word-style prompts, character-level text accuracy, semantic similarity, and CLIP visual-context alignment.
- [The role of task breadth and cognitive flexibility in generative AI-supported creative idea generation](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.technovation.2026.103540): Tests how task breadth and human cognitive flexibility condition the creative value of GenAI-supported idea generation.
- [DEVELOPING COMPUTATIONAL AESTHETICS FRAMEWORKS TO EVALUATE QUALITY IN DIGITAL ARTWORK CREATIONS](https://doi.org/10.29121/shodhkosh.v7.i4s.2026.7474): Proposes a computational-aesthetics framework for evaluating digital artwork quality, connecting AI art assessment with measurable aesthetic attributes instead of purely subjective critique.
