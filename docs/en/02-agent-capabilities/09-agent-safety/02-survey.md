# 2.9.2 Survey

- [Prompt Injection Attacks on Large Language Models: A Survey of Attack Methods, Root Causes, and Defense Strategies](https://www.techscience.com/cmc/v87n1/66084/html):Following Kitchenham's systematic-review guidelines, the survey synthesizes 128 peer-reviewed studies from 2022 to 2025, tracing prompt injection from direct text attacks to multimodal variants. It reports attack success above 90% on unprotected systems, catalogs 37 defenses in three categories, and finds that defenses reaching up to 95% protection on known patterns still leave major gaps for novel attacks and standardized evaluation.
- [Artificial Intelligence Trust, Risk and Security Management (AI TRiSM): Frameworks, applications, challenges and future research directions](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eswa.2023.122442): Reviews AI TRiSM across risk governance, trust management, security controls, monitoring, and assurance practice for enterprise AI deployments.
- [Revisiting Zero-Shot Abstractive Summarization in the Era of Large Language Models from the Perspective of Position Bias](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01989): Analyzes position bias in zero-shot LLM summarization, making evaluation-order effects visible for safety and reliability audits.
- [Risk Taxonomy, Mitigation, and Assessment Benchmarks of Large Language Model Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05778): Provides a risk taxonomy, mitigation map, and benchmark overview for LLM systems.
- [Two types of AI existential risk: decisive and accumulative](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07836): Distinguishes sudden decisive catastrophe from slower accumulative risk, clarifying which mitigation arguments apply to each existential-risk pathway.
- [From GPT-4 to Gemini and Beyond: Assessing the Landscape of MLLMs on Generalizability, Trustworthiness and Causality through Four Modalities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15071): Uses 230 manually designed text, code, image, and video cases to compare GPT-4, Gemini, and open MLLMs on generalizability, trustworthiness, and causal reasoning.
- [Recent Advances in Online Hate Speech Moderation: Multimodality and the Role of Large Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16727): Reviews hate-speech moderation across textual, visual, and auditory signals, emphasizing how LLMs and LMMs reshape detection, low-resource coverage, and governance gaps.
- [Not My Voice! A Taxonomy of Ethical and Safety Harms of Speech Generators](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01708): Builds a relational taxonomy of speech-generation harms from incident analysis, separating harms by affected-person exposure and creator or deployer motives.
