# 1.1.3 Bench

- [XNLI](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.05053): evaluates cross-lingual natural language inference (NLI). The core idea is to extend inference questions (entailment/contradiction/neutral) to multilingual settings and measure cross-lingual semantic alignment and reasoning consistency.
- [TyDi QA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05002) ([leaderboard](https://github.com/google-research-datasets/tydiqa/blob/master/leaderboard.md)): evaluates multilingual question answering, with more emphasis on the authenticity and linguistic diversity of "native-language questions." The core idea is to cover typologically more diverse languages and measure retrieval/understanding robustness under a QA protocol.
- [FLORES-200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04672) ([open-source code](https://github.com/facebookresearch/flores)): evaluates large-scale multilingual machine translation and cross-lingual generation. The core idea is to use broad language coverage and standardized references to measure multilingual generation quality and cross-lingual transfer.
- [German Text Embedding Clustering Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02709): evaluates German text-embedding clustering across domains, clustering algorithms, mono- and multilingual encoders, dimensionality reduction, and continued pretraining effects for short German texts.
- [MiTTenS: A Dataset for Evaluating Gender Mistranslation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06935): evaluates gender mistranslation when translating into and out of English across 26 languages, using handcrafted failure-pattern passages, synthetic long passages, and natural-domain text to compare MT systems and foundation models.
- [MAPLE: Multilingual Evaluation of Parameter Efficient Finetuning of Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07598): evaluates PEFT on Llama-2-7B and Mistral-7B using two synthetic multilingual instruction-tuning datasets and six downstream tasks across 40 languages, measuring whether low-rank adaptation narrows English and non-English performance gaps.
- [The Neglected Tails in Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12425): measures long-tail visual concept exposure by counting synonym occurrences in VLM pretraining text with LLMs, then tests CLIP, GPT-4V, and Stable Diffusion on rare concepts where recognition or generation often collapses.
- [MT-Ranker: Reference-free machine translation evaluation by inter-system ranking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17099): reframes reference-free MT evaluation as pairwise inter-system ranking: given a source sentence and two candidate translations, the evaluator predicts which translation is better and reports correlation with human judgments.
- [CIDAR: Culturally Relevant Instruction Dataset For Arabic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03177): contains 10,000 Arabic instruction-output pairs reviewed for cultural alignment with the Arab region, supporting instruction-following evaluation and tuning beyond translated English-centric datasets.
- [KMMLU: Measuring Massive Multitask Language Understanding in Korean](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11548): builds KMMLU with 35,030 expert-level Korean multiple-choice questions across 45 subjects from original Korean exams, evaluating 27 public and proprietary LLMs without relying on translated English benchmarks.
- [NEO-BENCH: Evaluating Robustness of Large Language Models with Neologisms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12261): stress-tests temporal language drift with recent English neologisms, comparing sentences that contain new words against near-identical substitute-word sentences across MT, NLU tasks, and perplexity.
- [ArabicMMLU: Assessing Massive Multitask Language Understanding in Arabic](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12840) ([Open Arabic LLM Leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/spaces/OALL/Open-Arabic-LLM-Leaderboard)): evaluates Arabic multitask language understanding with MMLU-style subject questions and an Open Arabic LLM Leaderboard, making Arabic proficiency visible beyond translated English benchmarks.
- [The Impact of Demonstrations on Multilingual In-Context Learning: A Multidimensional Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12976): analyzes multilingual in-context demonstrations across 5 model families, 9 classification and generation datasets, and 56 typologically diverse languages, separating demo quality from prompt-template effects.
- [Enhanced Hallucination Detection in Neural Machine Translation through Simple Detector Aggregation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13331): combines complementary hallucination detectors for neural machine translation, testing whether simple detector aggregation catches hallucinated translations more reliably than individual detectors.
- [OMGEval: An Open Multilingual Generative Evaluation Benchmark for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13524): provides 804 human-verified open-ended questions per language in Chinese, Russian, French, Spanish, and Arabic, using localized prompts and GPT-4-as-judge scoring for generative LLM evaluation.
- [Multi-FAct: Assessing Multilingual LLMs' Multi-Regional Knowledge using FActScore](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18045): extends FActScore-style long-form factuality evaluation to multilingual LLM outputs, using non-English Wikipedia and regionally diverse topics to test factual accuracy beyond English generation.
- [An Improved Traditional Chinese Evaluation Suite for Foundation Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01858): introduces TMMLU+, a Traditional Chinese multiple-choice benchmark with 66 subjects from elementary to professional level, six times larger and more balanced than TMMLU, and evaluates closed plus 26 open Chinese LLMs.
- [IndicVoices: Towards building an Inclusive Multilingual Speech Dataset for Indian Languages](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01926): releases IndicVoices, 7,348 hours of natural Indian-language speech from 16,237 speakers across 145 districts and 22 languages, with 1,639 transcribed hours plus standardized collection and transcription protocols.
- [Evaluating the Elementary Multilingual Capabilities of Large Language Models with MultiQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03814): provides 27.4k open-ended QA questions across 137 languages and reports both answer accuracy and language fidelity, separating whether a model knows the answer from whether it responds in the prompted language.
- [A Measure for Transparent Comparison of Linguistic Diversity in Multilingual NLP Data Sets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03909): proposes a feature-set Jaccard diversity score against a reference language sample, using typological databases and text-derived features to reveal which structural language types a dataset misses.
- [SeeGULL Multilingual: a Dataset of Geo-Culturally Situated Stereotypes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05696): builds SeeGULL Multilingual, a stereotype dataset with over 25k geo-culturally situated stereotypes across 20 languages and human annotations from 23 regions for multilingual safety and fairness evaluation.
- [GlossLM: A Massively Multilingual Corpus and Pretrained Model for Interlinear Glossed Text](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06399): compiles over 450k interlinear-glossed-text examples across 1.8k languages, normalizes morphosyntactic labels, and evaluates IGT generation for cross-lingual transfer and language documentation.
- [CLIcK: A Benchmark Dataset of Cultural and Linguistic Intelligence in Korean](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.06412): uses 1,995 Korean QA pairs from official exams and textbooks, split into language and culture categories with fine-grained knowledge annotations, to evaluate 13 language models on Korean cultural and linguistic intelligence.
- [Is Context Helpful for Chat Translation Evaluation?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08314): meta-evaluates automatic metrics for machine-translated chat, comparing reference-based and reference-free metrics and showing when conversational context improves correlation with human judgments, especially outside English directions.
- [DIALECTBENCH: A NLP Benchmark for Dialects, Varieties, and Closely-Related Languages](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11009): aggregates 10 text-level NLP task families over 281 dialects, varieties, and closely related languages to measure performance gaps between standard and non-standard language varieties.
- [Lost in Translation? Translation Errors and Challenges for Fair Assessment of Text-to-Image Models on Multilingual Concepts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11092): audits CoCo-CroLa multilingual text-to-image prompts by correcting translation errors in Spanish, Japanese, and Chinese, then reassesses baseline T2I models and predicts image-benchmark impact from text-similarity scores.
- [A New Massive Multilingual Dataset for High-Performance Language Technologies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14009): releases HPLT multilingual web resources: 75-language monolingual corpora with about 5.6 trillion deduplicated word tokens and an English-centric parallel corpus over 18 language pairs with 96M aligned sentence pairs.
- [KazQAD: Kazakh Open-Domain Question Answering Dataset](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04487): releases a Kazakh ODQA benchmark with nearly 6k unique questions, about 12k passage relevance judgments, an 800k-passage Kazakh Wikipedia corpus, and retrieval, reading-comprehension, and full-ODQA baselines.
- [MLaKE: Multilingual Knowledge Editing Benchmark for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04990): evaluates multilingual knowledge editing with 4,072 multi-hop and 5,360 single-hop questions in English, Chinese, Japanese, French, and German, using free-form and multiple-choice fact-chain queries.
- [Khayyam Challenge (PersianMMLU): Is Your LLM Truly Wise to The Persian Language?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06644): creates Khayyam Challenge or PersianMMLU with 20,192 four-choice Persian questions from 38 exam-derived tasks, spanning school levels, subjects, difficulty metadata, human response rates, and descriptive answers.
- [XNLIeu: a dataset for cross-lingual NLI in Basque](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06996): extends XNLI to Basque through machine translation followed by professional post-editing, then compares monolingual and multilingual LLM strategies for Basque NLI and the effect of translation-built test data.
- [Heron-Bench: A Benchmark for Evaluating Vision Language Models in Japanese](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07824): evaluates Japanese VLM capability with image-question-answer pairs tailored to Japanese context, reporting ability-dimension gaps between a Japanese baseline VLM and stronger closed models such as GPT-4V.
- [Quantifying Multilingual Performance of Large Language Models Across Languages](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.11553): Measures large language model performance across languages, providing evidence for multilingual capability gaps.
- [NormAd: A Framework for Measuring the Cultural Adaptability of Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12464): introduces NormAd and NormAd-Eti, a 2.6k-situation cultural-adaptability benchmark covering social-etiquette norms from 75 countries and judging LLM acceptability across abstract values and explicit norms.
- [CultureBank: An Online Community-Driven Knowledge Base Towards Culturally Aware Language Technologies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15238): builds cultural descriptors and scenarios from 12k TikTok and 11k Reddit self-narratives, then uses the resulting CultureBank to evaluate LLM cultural awareness and zero-shot transfer after fine-tuning.
- [WorldValuesBench: A Large-Scale Benchmark Dataset for Multi-Cultural Value Awareness of Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16308): derives more than 20 million value-prediction examples from World Values Survey responses by 94,728 participants, asking models to infer value ratings from demographic context and survey questions.
- [IndicGenBench: A Multilingual Benchmark to Evaluate Generation Capabilities of LLMs on Indic Languages](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16816): covers 29 Indic languages across 13 scripts and 4 language families with human-curated generation tasks, including cross-lingual summarization, machine translation, and cross-lingual QA.
- [Evaluation of geographical distortions in language models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17401): introduces four indicators that compare geographical and semantic distances, testing ten language models for spatial-knowledge distortions and geography-related representation bias.
- [Evaluation of Few-Shot Learning for Classification Tasks in the Polish Language](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17832): defines a native Polish few-shot classification benchmark with seven tasks, comparing 0-shot and 16-shot fine-tuning, linear probing, SetFit, and in-context learning across commercial and open models.
- [101 Billion Arabic Words Dataset](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.01590): mines and cleans 101 billion Arabic words from Common Crawl WET files to reduce Arabic LLM dependence on translated English data and supply original regional Arabic text for training and evaluation.
- [Bridging the Bosphorus: Advancing Turkish Large Language Models through Strategies for Low-Resource Language Adaptation and Benchmarking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04685): Bridging the Bosphorus: studies Turkish low-resource LLM adaptation by comparing English-pretrained model adaptation with Turkish-from-scratch pretraining, then benchmarking Turkish instruction tuning and language capability under data-scarcity constraints.
- [From Human Judgements to Predictive Models: Unravelling Acceptability in Code-Mixed Sentences](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05572): Studies human judgments and predictive models for acceptability in code-mixed sentences.
- [Evaluating Dialect Robustness of Language Models via Conversation Understanding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05688): extends MD3 into M-MD3 for US and Indian English conversation understanding, using target-word prediction and selection tasks to measure dialect robustness gaps in Llama3, GPT-4, and GPT-3.5.
- [PL-MTEB: Polish Massive Text Embedding Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.10138): introduces PL-MTEB with 30 Polish embedding tasks across classification, clustering, pair classification, information retrieval, and semantic textual similarity, evaluating 30 Polish and multilingual embedding models.
- [FAME-MT Dataset: Formality Awareness Made Easy for Machine Translation Purposes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11942): releases FAME-MT, 11.2M formality-labeled translations across 112 European language pairs, enabling MT systems to steer formal versus informal target-language register.
- [Tagengo: A Multilingual Chat Dataset](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12612): contributes more than 70k prompt-response pairs in 74 languages and evaluates multilingual chat fine-tuning on MT-Bench variants in six languages, showing benefits from broad multilingual data over single-target-language tuning.
- [M4U: Evaluating Multilingual Understanding and Reasoning for Large Multimodal Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15638): uses 10k multilingual multimodal samples over 64 disciplines and 16 subfields in science, engineering, and healthcare across six languages, where GPT-4o reaches only 47.6% average accuracy.
- [Extending the Massive Text Embedding Benchmark to French](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20468): extends MTEB to French sentence embeddings with 15 existing datasets, 3 new French datasets, 8 task categories, 51 evaluated embedding models, statistical tests, and a public leaderboard.
- [Open Ko-LLM Leaderboard: Evaluating Large Language Models in Korean with Ko-H5 Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20574) ([results](https://huggingface.co/datasets/open-ko-llm-leaderboard/results)): describes the Open Ko-LLM Leaderboard and Ko-H5 Benchmark for Korean LLM evaluation, using private test sets, leakage analysis, score-correlation studies, and temporal leaderboard tracking.
- [clembench-2024: A Challenging, Dynamic, Complementary, Multilingual Benchmark and Underlying Flexible Framework for LLMs as Multi-Action Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20859): scores LLMs in self-play conversational games for instruction following, strategic goal orientation, and language understanding, using a dynamic game framework that can vary prompting language and reduce contamination.
- [The Scandinavian Embedding Benchmarks: Comprehensive Assessment of Multilingual and Monolingual Text Embedding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02396): integrates Scandinavian text-embedding evaluation into MTEB with 24 tasks, 10 subtasks, and 4 task categories, comparing more than 26 public and commercial models.
- [IrokoBench: A New Benchmark for African Languages in the Age of Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03368): introduces IrokoBench, a human-translated benchmark for 17 low-resource African languages covering NLI, mathematical reasoning, and multiple-choice knowledge QA under zero-shot, few-shot, and translate-test settings.
- [Evaluating the IWSLT2023 Speech Translation Tasks: Human Annotations, Automatic Metrics, and Segmentation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03881): human-evaluates IWSLT 2023 speech-translation shared-task outputs using automatic resegmentation and direct assessment with segment context, then analyzes automatic metrics and segmentation effects.
- [mCSQA: Multilingual Commonsense Reasoning Dataset with Unified Creation Strategy by Language Models and Humans](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04215): creates mCSQA by combining language-model generation, refinement, verification, and human checks to build language-specific multilingual commonsense QA rather than translating English CommonsenseQA.
- [M3GIA: A Cognition Inspired Multilingual and Multimodal General Intelligence Ability Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05343): constructs a cognition-inspired multilingual multimodal benchmark around five CHC intelligence factors, spanning English, Chinese, French, Spanish, Portuguese, and Korean with native-context cultural data and human comparisons.
- [CVQA: Culturally-diverse Multilingual Visual Question Answering Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.05967): Benchmarks culturally diverse multilingual visual question answering.
- [LINGOLY: A Benchmark of Olympiad-Level Linguistic Reasoning Puzzles in Low-Resource and Extinct Languages](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06196): builds LingOly from 1,133 Linguistics Olympiad problems over more than 90 mostly low-resource or extinct languages, using six formats, five human difficulty levels, direct accuracy, and no-context baselines.
- [MultiPragEval: Multilingual Pragmatic Evaluation of Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07736): contains 1,200 pragmatics question units in English, German, Korean, and Chinese, organized by Grice's conversational maxims to test contextual awareness and implied-meaning inference.
- [Linguistic Bias in ChatGPT: Language Models Reinforce Dialect Discrimination](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08818): probes GPT-3.5 Turbo and GPT-4 with speaker text from ten English dialects, combining linguistic feature annotation and native-speaker evaluation to measure stereotyping, demeaning content, and standard-dialect defaulting.
- [BLEnD: A Benchmark for LLMs on Everyday Knowledge in Diverse Cultures and Languages](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09948): Benchmarks everyday knowledge across diverse cultures and languages.
- [SEACrowd: A Multilingual Multimodal Data Hub and Benchmark Suite for Southeast Asian Languages](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10118): consolidates SEACrowd as a Southeast Asian data hub covering nearly 1,000 languages across text, image, and audio, and benchmarks 36 indigenous SEA languages over 13 tasks.
- [DevBench: A multimodal developmental benchmark for language learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10215): compares vision-language models with child and adult behavioral data over seven lexical, syntactic, and semantic language tasks, measuring both accuracy and human-like response patterns.
- [Error Span Annotation: A Balanced Approach for Human Evaluation of Machine Translation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11580): defines Error Span Annotation for MT human evaluation by combining direct-assessment style continuous scores with MQM-style error severity spans, aiming to reduce expert-only annotation cost.
- [AI-Assisted Human Evaluation of Machine Translation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12419): adds ESA-AI for MT human evaluation, pre-filling error spans with recall-oriented quality estimation so annotators keep ESA-level quality while cutting span-annotation time from 71 to 31 seconds.
- [Can Large Language Models Code Like a Linguist?: A Case Study in Low Resource Sound Law Induction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12725): casts low-resource sound-law induction as programming-by-examples, evaluating LLM-generated Python sound-change programs against existing automated SLI methods and synthetic-data fine-tuning variants.
- [Chumor 1.0: A Truly Funny and Challenging Chinese Humor Understanding Dataset from Ruo Zhi Ba](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12754): constructs Chumor from culturally specific Ruo Zhi Ba Chinese jokes, annotates explanations, and uses native-speaker A/B tests to show human explanations beat GPT-4o and ERNIE Bot.
- [Is It Good Data for Multilingual Instruction Tuning or Just Bad Multilingual Evaluation for Large Language Models?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12822): separates native versus translated multilingual instruction data and native versus translated test sets, showing which benchmark types reveal translation defects and language-specific knowledge gaps in multilingual tuning.
- [On the Evaluation Practices in Multilingual NLP: Can Machine Translation Offer an Alternative to Human Translations?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14267): translates four multilingual NLP test tasks into 198 languages with a state-of-the-art MT model, probing when machine-translated test data overestimates low-resource and unseen-language performance.
- [MultiTalk: Enhancing 3D Talking Head Generation Across Languages with Multilingual Video Dataset](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14272): introduces a multilingual talking-head generation task with over 420 hours of videos in 20 languages, language-specific style embeddings, and a multilingual lip-sync accuracy metric.
- [Leveraging LLMs for Bangla Grammar Error Correction: Error Categorization, Synthetic Data, and Model Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14284): categorizes 12 Bangla grammatical error classes, collects native-speaker error evidence, and creates the Vaiyakarana dataset of 567,422 noisy-clean sentence pairs through rule-based noise injection.
- [ExU: AI Models for Examining Multilingual Disinformation Narratives and Understanding their Spread](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15443): frames multilingual disinformation analysis around rumour stance classification and claim retrieval, grounding the tool design in fact-checker and journalist user requirements.
- [Disce aut Deficere: Evaluating LLMs Proficiency on the INVALSI Italian Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17535): adapts Italy's INVALSI educational tests for automated LLM evaluation and compares model proficiency against human results across Italian school-competency assessments.
- [Spanish and LLM Benchmarks: Is MMLU Lost in Translation?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17789): studies Spanish MMLU translation quality, showing that scores on translated benchmark categories mix model ability with translation artifacts and cultural bias from source English items.
- [PrExMe! Large Scale Prompt Exploration of Open Source LLMs for Machine Translation and Summarization Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18528): introduces PrExMe, a large-scale prompt-exploration protocol for using open LLMs as MT and summarization evaluators in low-resource or time-constrained settings.
- [From Local Concepts to Universals: Evaluating the Multicultural Understanding of Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00263): introduces GlobalRG with retrieval-across-universals over culturally diverse images from 50 countries and cultural visual grounding over culture-specific concepts from 15 countries.
- [Improving Multilingual Instruction Finetuning via Linguistically Natural and Diverse Datasets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01853): creates linguistically natural multilingual instruction-tuning data from monolingual corpora, English-focused LLMs, and a scoring function, improving multilingual summarization by 17.57% over translation-based data and 15.23% over template data.
- [GPT-4 vs. Human Translators: A Comprehensive Evaluation of Translation Quality Across Languages, Domains, and Expertise Levels](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03658): compares GPT-4 with junior, medium, and senior human translators across multiple language pairs and domains through annotation rounds that count errors and reveal weaker low-resource and domain-specific translation performance.
- [M5 - A Diverse Benchmark to Assess the Performance of Large Multimodal Models Across Multilingual and Multicultural Vision-Language Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.03791): builds M5 with eight datasets over five vision-language task types and 41 languages, emphasizing underrepresented languages and culturally diverse images for multilingual multimodal evaluation.
- [NADI 2024: The Fifth Nuanced Arabic Dialect Identification Shared Task](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.04910): Defines the fifth Nuanced Arabic Dialect Identification shared task for evaluating dialectal Arabic language understanding.
- [A Principled Framework for Evaluating on Typologically Diverse Languages](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.05022): proposes a typology-informed language-sampling framework and diversity metrics for selecting representative multilingual evaluation languages when exhaustive coverage is infeasible.
- [An Empirical Study of Gendered Stereotypes in Emotional Attributes for Bangla in Multilingual Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06432): probes closed and open LLMs for Bangla gendered emotion attribution, testing stereotypes around empathy, fear, guilt, anger, bravado, and authority in a low-resource language context.
- [NativQA: Multilingual Culturally-Aligned Natural Query for LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09823): constructs MultiNativQA with roughly 64k manually annotated QA pairs in seven languages, using native-speaker queries from nine regions and 18 topics to evaluate culturally aligned natural QA.
- [Building pre-train LLM Dataset for the INDIC Languages: a case study on Hindi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09855): curates a 1.28-billion-token Hindi pretraining corpus spanning multiple domains and major Hindi dialects, documenting a data-collection and preprocessing pipeline for Indic LLM development.
- [Benchmarking Vision Language Models for Cultural Understanding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10920): builds CulturalVQA with 2,378 image-question pairs, one to five answers per question, and cultural facets from 11 countries on five continents such as clothing, food, drinks, rituals, and traditions.
- [Empowering Persian LLMs for Instruction Following: A Novel Dataset and Training Approach](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11186): introduces FarsInstruct and a Persian instruction-tuning pipeline, using native Persian data and training variants to evaluate low-resource instruction following rather than translated English prompts only.
- [M2DS: Multilingual Dataset for Multi-document Summarisation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12336): creates multilingual multi-document summarization data from BBC multilingual news, pairing source document sets with summaries in five languages to move MDS evaluation beyond English-only collections.
- [TurkishMMLU: Measuring Massive Multitask Language Understanding in Turkish](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12402): uses more than 10,000 expert-written Turkish high-school questions across 9 subjects, evaluating open and closed LLMs under zero-shot, few-shot, chain-of-thought, and difficulty-analysis protocols.
- [SpeciaLex: A Benchmark for In-Context Specialized Lexicon Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13297): tests in-context learning of specialized lexicon constraints with 1,785 instances across 18 subtasks, covering checking, identification, rewriting, and open generation.
- [INDIC QA BENCHMARK: A Multilingual Benchmark to Evaluate Question Answering capability of LLMs for Indic Languages](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13522): evaluates context-grounded question answering in 11 major Indian languages, covering both extractive and abstractive settings and comparing multilingual LLMs with a translate-test pipeline.
- [Long Input Benchmark for Russian Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02439): defines LIBRA, a Russian long-input benchmark for analyzing whether LLMs can process long Russian documents and extended token sequences rather than short translated tasks.
- [MultiHateClip: A Multilingual Benchmark Dataset for Hateful Video Detection on YouTube and Bilibili](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03468): releases MultiHateClip for multilingual hateful-video detection on YouTube and Bilibili, extending hate-speech evaluation from text-only settings to multimodal video evidence.
- [Speech-MASSIVE: A Multilingual Speech Dataset for SLU and Beyond](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03900): creates Speech-MASSIVE, a multilingual spoken-language-understanding dataset derived from MASSIVE that supports intent and slot evaluation for speech models across many languages.
- [Hindi-BEIR : A Large Scale Retrieval Benchmark in Hindi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.09437): builds a Hindi retrieval benchmark from translated BEIR subsets, existing Hindi IR data, and synthetic retrieval datasets, covering 15 datasets across 8 tasks with multilingual retriever and reranker baselines.
- [Benchmarking LLMs for Translating Classical Chinese Poetry:Evaluating Adequacy, Fluency, and Elegance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.09945): uses PoetMT, where each classical Chinese poem has a recognized elegant translation, and scores adequacy, fluency, and elegance with GPT-4-based, BLEU, COMET, BLEURT, and human evaluations.
- [Event Stream-based Sign Language Translation: A High-Definition Benchmark Dataset and A Novel Baseline](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10488): Evaluates event Stream-based Sign Language Translation: introduces Event-CSL, a high-definition event-camera sign-language translation benchmark with 14,827 videos, 14,821 glosses, 2,544 Chinese text words, and varied scenes, viewpoints, lighting, and camera motion.
- [Expanding FLORES+ Benchmark for more Low-Resource Settings: Portuguese-Emakhuwa Machine Translation Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11457): extends FLORES+ dev and devtest from Portuguese into Emakhuwa with post-editing, adequacy checks, multiple references, and baseline NMT and multilingual MT evaluations.
- [The Russian-focused embedders' exploration: ruMTEB benchmark and Russian embedding model design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.12503): extends MTEB into ruMTEB for Russian embeddings, covering seven task categories such as semantic textual similarity, classification, reranking, and retrieval with an open framework and leaderboard.
- [Guardians of the Machine Translation Meta-Evaluation: Sentinel Metrics Fall In!](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.13831): introduces sentinel metrics to audit WMT machine-translation metric meta-evaluation, testing ranking robustness, fairness, and bias toward metrics trained to mimic human assessments or output continuous scores.
- [Native vs Non-Native Language Prompting: A Comparative Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07054): Compares native and non-native language prompting behavior.
- [Cracking the Code: Multi-domain LLM Evaluation on Real-World Professional Exams in Indonesia](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08564): introduces IndoCareer with 8,834 Indonesian professional-certification multiple-choice questions across healthcare, finance, creative, tourism, education, and law to test local vocational knowledge.
- [L3Cube-IndicQuest: A Benchmark Question Answering Dataset for Evaluating Knowledge of LLMs in Indic Context](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08706): creates L3Cube-IndicQuest with 200 factual QA pairs for English and each of 19 Indic languages across five Indic-region domains, testing regional knowledge in multilingual LLMs.
- [DynamicNER: A Dynamic, Multilingual, and Fine-Grained Dataset for LLM-based Named Entity Recognition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11022): builds DynamicNER as a multilingual fine-grained named-entity-recognition dataset for LLM-based NER, emphasizing dynamic entity categories and multilingual annotation rather than fixed English-only schemas.
- [RoMath: A Mathematical Reasoning Benchmark in Romanian](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11074): introduces RoMath, a Romanian mathematical-reasoning benchmark that tests LLM problem solving in Romanian rather than inferring ability from English math benchmarks.
- [AraDiCE: Benchmarks for Dialectal and Cultural Capabilities in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11404): Benchmarks dialectal and cultural capabilities for language models in Arabic-centered settings.
- [Linguini](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12126) ([open-source code](https://github.com/facebookresearch/linguini), [dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/facebook/linguini)): evaluates language-agnostic linguistic reasoning.
