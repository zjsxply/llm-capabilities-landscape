# 3.2.2 Survey

- [Interpretable Scientific Discovery with Symbolic Regression: A Review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.10873): This review organizes interpretable scientific discovery through symbolic regression by contrasting genetic-programming and deep-learning families and spelling out their respective strengths and failure modes.
- [Discovering Causal Relations and Equations from Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13341): The survey connects observational causal discovery with equation discovery, showing how invariance, interventions, domain knowledge, and modern machine learning jointly support interpretable physical-law discovery.
- [Toward the Transparent Use of Generative Artificial Intelligence in Academic Articles](https://doi.org/10.3138/jsp-2023-0053): The work organizes AI-assisted scholarship into issue development, question answering, and findings communication, and proposes tiered disclosure rules whose required transparency depends on how AI was used.
- [Potential of artificial intelligence in textual cohesion, grammatical precision, and clarity in scientific writing](https://doi.org/10.62486/latia2024110): Applies the SALSA method in a qualitative systematic review of studies published since 2023 and indexed by Scopus, Web of Science, SciELO, and Latindex; the review finds that AI can improve cohesion, grammatical precision, and clarity, especially as a support tool, but that effectiveness depends on the use context and appropriate human involvement, with ethical and legal challenges remaining.
- [ChatGPT y comunicación científica: hacia un uso de la Inteligencia Artificial que sea tan útil como responsable](https://doi.org/10.31009/hipertext.net.2023.i26.03): Examines ChatGPT across research design, data collection, and manuscript writing through existing application examples, then turns the observed opportunities and limitations into practical requirements for honest disclosure, transparent use, research integrity, ethical judgment, and continued human critical oversight.
- [Decoding complexity: how machine learning is redefining scientific discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.04161):The survey organizes how machine learning supports scientific discovery when the underlying phenomenon is known to different degrees, using examples such as brain mapping and exoplanet detection. It argues that large scientific datasets and literature enable more complex models, while emphasizing rigorous validation, interpretability, and the risks of over-reliance on ML in discovery claims.
- [Automating the Practice of Science - Opportunities, Challenges, and Implications](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05890):This article surveys where scientific automation can accelerate discovery and reproducibility, while organizing current bottlenecks and ethical or practical consequences that constrain deployment.
- [An Analysis of Tasks and Datasets in Peer Reviewing](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2024.sdp-1.24): Inventories peer-review automation tasks and their datasets, separating reviewer matching, score prediction, argument mining, review generation, decision prediction, and meta-review support so later systems can be compared against the right subtask rather than a vague “AI reviewer” label.
- [Risks of AI scientists: prioritizing safeguarding over autonomy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04247): This perspective maps AI-scientist vulnerabilities by user intent, research domain, and external impact, then proposes a three-part safeguard of human regulation, agent alignment, and feedback-aware agent regulation.
- [AI-driven research in pure mathematics and theoretical physics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19973): Organizes AI-assisted theoretical discovery into top-down, bottom-up, and meta-mathematics approaches, comparing recent progress with the limits that still keep human theorists in the loop.
- [A Comprehensive Survey of Scientific Large Language Models and Their Applications in Scientific Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10833): Maps more than 260 scientific LLMs across modalities, comparing their architectures, pre-training corpora, evaluations, and uses in scientific discovery rather than treating each scientific discipline as an isolated model family.
- [Data Analysis in the Era of Generative AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18475): Frames AI-assisted data analysis around translating high-level intent into executable code, charts, and insights, then ties usable systems to interaction design, trust, benchmarking, and user-needs gaps.
- [Towards Scientific Discovery with Generative AI: Progress, Opportunities, and Challenges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11427): Reviews progress toward autonomous long-horizon scientific discovery and identifies the missing ingredients: science-focused agents, stronger benchmarks, multimodal scientific representations, theorem proving, and integrated data-driven modelling.
- [Virtual laboratories: transforming research with AI](https://doi.org/10.1017/dce.2024.15): Surveys how AI-enabled virtual laboratories transform research workflows.
- [Paradigm shifts: exploring AI's influence on qualitative inquiry and analysis](https://doi.org/10.3389/frma.2024.1331589):The article argues that rule-based automation can accelerate qualitative coding but may conflict with interpretivist assumptions and miss communication nuance. It proposes partial automation in which AI supports rigorous, rapid analysis while the human researcher retains contextual understanding and final interpretation, a concrete governance principle for qualitative research workflows.
- [Appliances of Generative AI-Powered Language Tools in Academic Writing: A Scoping Review](https://doi.org/10.17323/jle.2024.24181): A PRISMA/PPC review of 44 studies groups academic-writing applications into enhancement, challenges, and authorship/integrity, while documenting concrete uses such as proofreading, annotation, paraphrase, translation, and feedback alongside hallucinated references and loss of author voice.
- [Regulatory solutions to alleviate the risks of generative AI models in qualitative research](https://doi.org/10.1080/17516234.2024.2399098): Builds risk scenarios from a publication review, a workshop with four qualitative researchers, and interviews with seven reviewers, researchers, and respondents, then compares policies in five jurisdictions and arranges interventions from soft law to hard law.
- [Unmasking artificial intelligence (AI): Identifying articles written by AI models](https://doi.org/10.18231/j.ijca.2024.028): Organizes AI-written-article identification into technical analysis, metadata checks, stylometry, coherence testing, and detector models such as GPTZero, while identifying duplicity, validity, responsibility, and authorship credit as the associated integrity risks.
- [Evaluación del uso ético de ChatGPT en investigación científica: principales tendencias, mejores prácticas y desafíos identificados](https://doi.org/10.3145/thinkepi.2024.e18a19): A scoping review groups the evidence into ethical ChatGPT use in academic research, education and health, and bias, equity, and regulation, then identifies authorship, citation, transparency, and longitudinal ethical oversight as recurring controls.
- [How Can Generative AI (GenAI) Enhance or Hinder Qualitative Studies? A Critical Appraisal from South Asia, Nepal](https://doi.org/10.46743/2160-3715/2024.6637): The appraisal organizes GenAI use in qualitative studies around co-authorship, conversational support, and research-assistant functions, then pairs those roles with reliability, bias, and acknowledgment safeguards for human-led analysis.
- [Ethical reflections on the use of Generative Artificial Intelligence in the academic sphere: writing and authorship](https://doi.org/10.5753/wics.2024.3170): A broad literature review connects GenAI use in literature review, ideation, and content creation with authorship, plagiarism, and intellectual-property risks, and calls for academia, companies, and governments to define rules grounded in transparency, justice, equity, responsibility, and beneficence; the abstract reports no review corpus or synthesis protocol.
- [Scientific Hypothesis Generation and Validation: Methods, Datasets, and Future Directions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04651): The survey organizes scientific hypothesis systems from symbolic discovery through LLM, retrieval, knowledge-graph, simulation, causal, and multi-agent pipelines, while comparing how novelty, interpretability, and validation are traded off.
- [Bayesian symbolic regression: Automated equation discovery from a physicists' perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19540):explains probabilistic symbolic regression through information theory and statistical physics, contrasting its explicit model plausibility and ensemble-based uncertainty with heuristic search and regularization.
- [Large Language Model-based Data Science Agent: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02744): This survey unifies data-science-agent design around roles, execution, knowledge, and reflection, then maps those controls onto preprocessing, modeling, evaluation, and visualization stages.
- [More Parameters Than Populations: A Systematic Literature Review of Large Language Models within Survey Research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03391): A citation-network and database review organizes LLM use in survey research into pre-data collection, data collection, and post-data collection, then uses survey-methodology experience to identify data-quality prerequisites, opportunities, and failure modes.
- [A Systematic Survey on Large Language Models for Evolutionary Optimization: From Modeling to Solving](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08269): The survey separates LLM use in evolutionary optimization into modeling and solving, then distinguishes stand-alone optimizers, embedded components, and high-level algorithm managers.
- [Generative Large Language Models (gLLMs) in Content Analysis: A Practical Guide for Communication Research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24337): Synthesizes a seven-stage workflow for LLM-assisted quantitative content analysis, connecting codebook design, prompting, model configuration, iterative refinement, and reliability validation to established standards for validity, reproducibility, and research ethics.
- [Simulating Students with Large Language Models: A Review of Architecture, Mechanisms, and Role Modelling in Education with Generative AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.06078): This review treats LLM-driven simulated students as research instruments: it compares architectures and role-modelling mechanisms for emulating learner profiles, instructional responses, and multi-agent classroom behavior, then examines their use in curriculum evaluation and teacher training. Its distinctive research-automation axis is replacing difficult-to-scale learner studies with controllable synthetic participants while documenting bias, reliability, and alignment limits.
- [Presentation Generation for Scientific Research Papers: A Comprehensive Survey](https://doi.org/10.1109/ic-eeta66496.2025.11548444): Organizes paper-to-presentation systems from paper-content analysis and slide planning to scholarly visual design and evaluation, exposing the source grounding, narrative, and scientific-communication constraints that generic slide generators typically omit.
- [LLM4SR: A Survey on Large Language Models for Scientific Research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.04306): Surveys scientific literature, hypothesis generation, experimentation, data analysis, and writing.
- [Accelerating Discovery in Natural Science Laboratories with AI and Robotics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.06847): Synthesizes how AI and robotics can automate natural-science discovery, emphasizing reproducibility, throughput, standardization, and human oversight as shared laboratory constraints.
- [Large language models for automated scholarly paper review: A survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10326): Organizes automated scholarly paper review around deployed LLMs, solved bottlenecks, datasets, code, online systems, publisher reactions, and unresolved implementation risks.
- [Transforming Science with Large Language Models: A Survey on AI-assisted Scientific Discovery, Experimentation, Content Generation, and Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05151): Reviews AI-assisted scientific discovery from experimentation through content generation and evaluation.
- [The Design Space of Recent AI-assisted Research Tools](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16291): Surveys AI-assisted research tools for ideation, sensemaking, and scientific creativity, highlighting interaction patterns, automation risks, and design trade-offs.
- [From Hypothesis to Publication: A Comprehensive Survey of AI-Driven Research Support Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01424): Surveys AI support across hypothesis generation, experimentation, writing, review, and publication workflows.
- [Agentic AI for Scientific Discovery: A Survey of Progress, Challenges, and Future Directions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08979): Focuses on autonomous scientific workflows, tool use, and reliability challenges.
- [Towards Scientific Intelligence: A Survey of LLM-based Scientific Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.24047): Covers scientific-agent task types, architectures, benchmarks, and process automation.
- [A Survey on Hypothesis Generation for Scientific Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05496): Taxonomizes LLM-based hypothesis generation methods, quality-improvement techniques, and evaluation strategies for scientific discovery.
- [From Automation to Autonomy: A Survey on Large Language Models in Scientific Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13259): Surveys how LLM systems move from automating isolated scientific tasks toward autonomous discovery, organizing the field around the research-loop capabilities required for that transition.
- [Recalibrating the Compass: Integrating Large Language Models into Classical Research Methods](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19402): Surveys integrating large language models into classical research methods; useful for mapping methods, risks, and evaluation gaps in AI-assisted research workflows.
- [From Reasoning to Learning: A Survey on Hypothesis Discovery and Rule Learning with Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21935): Uses Peirce's abduction, deduction, and induction lens to organize LLM-based hypothesis generation, application, and validation as a path from information execution toward knowledge discovery.
- [Recent Advances and Future Directions in Literature-Based Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12385): Surveys literature-based discovery with knowledge graphs, deep learning, and LLMs for automated hypothesis generation.
- [AI4Research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01903): Maps AI support across the scientific lifecycle, from ideation and experimentation to analysis, writing, and review.
- [From AI for Science to Agentic Science: A Survey on Autonomous Scientific Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14111): Frames agentic science as a stage where LLMs, multimodal systems, and research platforms move from assistance to scientific agency across hypothesis generation, experiment design, execution, analysis, and iterative refinement.
- [Open Dataset Search in the LLM Era](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00728): Surveys dataset search under LLM-assisted workflows, adding a research-infrastructure view for finding and reusing data sources.
- [Autonomous Agents for Scientific Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09901): Frames LLM-based scientific agents around orchestration among human scientists, natural language, code, and physics across hypothesis, experiment, analysis, and refinement stages.
- [A Survey of AI Scientists](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23045): Use the six-stage framework to review the AI ​​scientist system, and separate Experimental Preparation and Experimental Execution from the end-to-end scientific research process to facilitate the identification of research gaps in experimental planning and preparation before execution.
- [A Tutorial on Generative AI and Simulation Modeling Integration](https://doi.org/10.1109/WSC68292.2025.11338995): Tutorializes how generative AI and LLMs can support simulation-model generation, execution, and analysis.
- [AI, agentic models and lab automation for scientific discovery — the beginning of scAInce](https://doi.org/10.3389/frai.2025.1649155): Reviews how agentic AI and automated labs can support scientific discovery workflows.
- [The Role of AI in Advancing Scientific Research from Hypothesis to Publication](https://doi.org/10.1109/aict67988.2025.11268778): A survey-style view of AI support across scientific research workflows. Core idea: connect hypothesis formation, analysis, writing, and publication support as one research lifecycle.
- [Scientific software development in the AI era: reproducibility, MLOps, and applications in soft matter physics](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphy.2025.1711356): A practitioner-oriented synthesis on scientific software development in the AI era. Core idea: connect reproducibility, MLOps, monitoring, and adaptive scientific modeling workflows.
- [Integrating Artificial Intelligence Tools for Research and Scholarly Communication in Academic Libraries: A Systematic Review](https://doi.org/10.1515/opis-2025-0057): Screening 226 records down to 24 studies, the review finds AI improves retrieval, analysis, and scholarly communication but exposes privacy, bias, and literacy gaps; it assigns librarians a continuing role in training and responsible adoption.
- [Generative AI in Academia: A Comprehensive Review of Applications and Implications for the Research Process.](https://doi.org/10.21608/ijeasou.2025.349520.1041): The review maps GenAI across ideation, literature review, data analysis, synthetic-data and hypothesis generation, and post-publication work, then pairs those opportunities with plagiarism, bias, privacy, and critical-thinking risks in a human-oversight framework.
- [¿Cómo está transformando la inteligencia artificial la comunicación científica? Desafíos, oportunidades y el papel de los actores involucrados: una revisión de alcance](https://doi.org/10.22201/iibi.24488321xe.2025.104.59032): A 90-study scoping review maps generative AI across scientific translation, manuscript drafting and editing, and peer review, while structuring the governance risks around bias, plagiarism, detection, policy gaps, and stakeholder duties.
- [The 2025 Landscape of Generative AI in Scholarly Writing and Publishing: A Scoping Review of Uses and Ethical Approaches](https://jle.hse.ru/article/view/29876):Following the Arksey-O'Malley framework and PRISMA-ScR, the review searched Scopus for English-language 2025 articles and reviews, screening 334 records and including 56. It organizes evidence around governance, content quality, and risk mitigation; maps uses in ideation, outlining, synthesis, language enhancement, peer review, and editorial work; and identifies emerging disclosure and task-taxonomy frameworks while emphasizing human accountability.
- [[Potentials and risks of using AI in qualitative research].](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.zefq.2025.10.004): The review treats LLMs as complementary second or third opinions for category development, coding, and interpretation, while arguing that methodological rigor, ethical judgment, data protection, and the core interpretive principles of qualitative research remain human responsibilities.
- [Plagiarism types and detection methods: a systematic survey of algorithms in text analysis](https://doi.org/10.3389/fcomp.2025.1504725): Organizes plagiarism detection around verbatim, paraphrase, translation, and idea-level misuse, then compares string matching, machine-learning, natural-language-processing, and deep-learning methods across cross-language, source-code, intrinsic, and AI-generated-content settings.
- [Artificial research participants in behavioral science](https://doi.org/10.1108/jeet-03-2025-0014): Separates two evidence-sensitive roles for LLM-simulated participants: complementing humans during data collection and replacing humans only during pilot testing, with implementation guidance for each role.
- [Multi-Modal Plagiarism Detection in the Era of AI: A Comprehensive Review and Novel Framework Proposal](https://doi.org/10.1109/acet67282.2025.11430248): A review of AI-era plagiarism detection proposes a seven-step multimodal framework combining OCR, transformer NLP, and lexical, semantic, and structural similarity analysis to cover text, scans, handwriting, paraphrase, and machine-generated content.
- [Agentic Quantitative Trading: A Survey of Workflows, Systems, and Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31041): The survey organizes agentic quantitative trading into factor mining, signal discovery, portfolio construction, order execution, and risk management, then compares strategy-construction, offline, live-market, and reliability benchmarks. It finds that current systems over-concentrate on signal discovery, rarely integrate portfolio, execution, and risk control end to end, and that forecasting strength does not reliably predict live trading performance.
- [Comparative Analysis of Traditional, Machine Learning, and LLM-based Data Preprocessing Methods](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICIRCA69024.2026.11570529): Compares predefined-rule preprocessing with adaptable machine-learning and LLM-based approaches, giving a cross-paradigm view of how data preparation methods have evolved for data-driven models.
- [AI as an accelerator for defining new problems that transcends boundaries](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13040-025-00429-w): Reviews interdisciplinary, transdisciplinary, convergence, and No-Boundary Thinking approaches, then argues that AI can synthesize cross-domain knowledge and expert perspectives to propose multiple candidate problem definitions for human-AI problem formulation.
- [AI-Generated Figures in Academic Publishing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16159): Surveys publisher policies and practical risks for AI-generated scientific figures, focusing on reproducibility, authorship attribution, visual misinformation, and disclosure requirements across major journal families.
- [A Visionary Look at Vibe Researching](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00945): Defines "vibe researching" as human-directed, agent-executed research work and maps its multi-agent, memory, tool-use, retrieval, limitation, and governance questions.
- [Can AI Be a Good Peer Reviewer?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27924): Overview of peer review process, review generation, rebuttal, meta-review, evaluation and ethical risks, which can be used as an overview reference for the direction of AI reviewer and peer review automation.
- [AutoResearch AI: Towards AI-Powered Research Automation for Scientific Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23204): Surveys the shift from task-level AI-for-science tools to workflow-level research automation, organizing literature grounding, hypothesis generation, experimentation, validation, reporting, and revision as one research-production pipeline.
- [Workflow Closure Is Not Scientific Closure in Auto-Research Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26200): Audits auto-research as an epistemic-control problem rather than a workflow-completion problem. Core idea: synthesize more than 100 papers and repositories plus 21 representative systems to diagnose objective, validation, and acceptance collapse, then argue for autonomous execution under non-autonomous scientific control.
- [From Text to Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08723): Surveys how LLMs reshape research across sciences and humanities. Core idea: organize LLM use in hypothesis generation, literature synthesis, data analysis, writing, and research-risk control across disciplines.

Scientific Discovery in the Age of Artificial Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17970):The paper surveys AI capabilities and scientometric diffusion in science, then proposes a typology from specialized scientific AI to assistants, agents, and hybrid experimental systems. It selectively reviews achievements across mathematics, computer science, physics, chemistry, life sciences, and social sciences while organizing technical, epistemic, institutional, and labor-division risks.
- [Leveraging Human-Aligned and Probabilistic AI for White-Box Research and Knowledge Discovery: A Survey](https://doi.org/10.1109/CSNT69054.2026.11502373): Reviews human-aligned and probabilistic AI for white-box research design and knowledge discovery.

- [AI Agents for the Science of Science: A Survey of Tasks, Architectures, Evaluations, and Challenges](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2026.findings-acl.1804): This survey organizes science-of-science agents by tasks, architectures, evaluations, and challenges, mapping the design space for computational scholarly research.

- [The Guideline for Reporting Generative Artificial Intelligence Use in Educational Research: Implications From a Systematic Review](https://doi.org/10.1109/tlt.2026.3708984): After screening 8,831 records and retaining 126 studies, the paper turns observed GenAI use from conceptualization through writing into a reporting guideline designed to make human involvement, methods, and responsible use explicit.
- [Silicon Samples: A Review and Outlook on Large Language Models Simulating Human Respondents](https://doi.org/10.23977/jaip.2026.090119): Reviewing 25 “silicon sample” studies, the paper organizes uses into four domains and evaluates them by psychometric validity, group fidelity, and context dependence, warning that anthropomorphic generalization and epistemic-justice risks limit substitution for human samples.
- [Navigating artificial intelligence in qualitative research: ethical and practical considerations from concept to publication](https://doi.org/10.3389/frma.2026.1863790): The review follows AI through five qualitative-research stages and argues that hallucination, privacy leakage, and loss of interpretive nuance require stage-specific documentation and human checks rather than a single generic AI-use rule.

- [Human-assisted traditional peer review intelligence vs artificial peer review intelligence: perspectives on future disruptive sciences](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44163-026-01013-9): Compares human-assisted peer-review intelligence with artificial peer-review intelligence, framing future peer review as a hybrid governance problem.
- [On the Vulnerability of Citation Metrics in the Era of Generative Artificial Intelligence](https://doi.org/10.3390/publications14020023): Analyzes how generative AI lowers the cost of publication-style manuscripts and creates new routes for manipulating citation metrics, making bibliometric robustness a publishing-governance issue.
- [LARGE LANGUAGE MODELS IN SCIENTIFIC RESEARCH WORKFLOWS: A SYSTEMATIC LITERATURE REVIEW OF APPLICATIONS, CHALLENGES, AND FUTURE DIRECTIONS](https://doi.org/10.64680/jisads.v4i1.65): This PRISMA review maps LLM use across knowledge discovery, study design, writing, and evaluation, and proposes an integration framework for reliable research workflows.
- [The past, present and future of self-driving laboratories](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41570-026-00847-2): Reviews the evolution, current architectures, and future directions of self-driving laboratories for autonomous experimentation.
- [A New Paradigm: Agentic AI for Scientific Discovery](https://doi.org/10.65649/ukh4n33x): Surveys agentic scientific discovery across a six-stage research lifecycle and frames verification as the central requirement for trustworthy autonomy.
- [Extended Inductive Thematic Analysis for the Digital Era: How Researchers Integrate AI and Traditional Digital Tools, and Why We Need a New Framework](https://doi.org/10.1177/16094069261473131): This study proposes an eight-stage AI-augmented inductive thematic-analysis framework that preserves human responsibility for interpretation, critique, and validation.
- [The rise of robotics in self-driving labs](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44182-026-00094-z): Surveys the robotic layer of self-driving laboratories, identifying how automation and algorithmic decision-making can broaden reproducible experimental design, execution, and analysis across disciplines while exposing the remaining scalability and generalization limits.
- [Research Acceleration in Self-Driving Labs](https://doi.org/10.1002/aisy.202200331): A technology roadmap for materials and molecule discovery is given, and automation hardware, experimental design strategies, data standards, active learning, and experimental closed-loop integration are regarded as key paths for the expansion of self-driven laboratories.
- [Exploring the role of large language models in the scientific method](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44387-025-00019-5): From hypothesis to discovery, discuss how LLM can be embedded in scientific methods, pointing out that experimental design and experimental execution automation must be co-designed with human goals, evaluation indicators, and safety boundaries.
- [Integrated Systems for Computational Scientific Discovery](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v38i20.30269):This survey frames integrated computational scientific discovery as a systems challenge, identifies eight component abilities, reviews prior partial integrations, and analyzes infrastructure and evaluation hurdles. It also proposes scientific domains where integrated computational artifacts should be tested, providing a field map rather than a Deep Research literature-synthesis workflow.
- [Transforming science labs into automated factories of discovery](https://doi.org/10.1126/scirobotics.adm6991):The article organizes laboratory automation from assistance to full automation and identifies robotics challenges tied to automation level and task generality across scientific discovery settings.
- [Symbolic Regression: A Pathway to Interpretability Towards Automated Scientific Discovery](https://doi.org/10.1145/3637528.3671464):The tutorial frames symbolic regression as direct equation learning for compact, interpretable models, contrasts major learning approaches, and discusses why physical-science settings expose its opportunities and limitations.
- [How to Harness the Power of GPT for Scientific Research: A Comprehensive Review of Methodologies, Applications, and Ethical Considerations](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13139-024-00876-z): The review treats prompt engineering as the control layer for GPT-assisted research and illustrates how prompts can support literature review, data analysis, and manuscript preparation, while insisting that efficiency gains remain bounded by human expertise and ethical checks.
- [Using large language models for survey research in communication: opportunities and challenges](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44382-025-00014-z): Maps LLM support for questionnaire development and open-response analysis, and pairs those use cases with validity, ethics, and synthetic-data risks that researchers must test before relying on AI-assisted surveys.
- [Getting Started with Artificial Intelligence Assisted Qualitative Analysis: An Introductory Guide to Qualitative Research Approaches with Exploratory Examples from Reflexive Content Analysis](https://doi.org/10.1177/16094069251354863): By contrasting Small-q and Big-Q qualitative traditions, the paper proposes an approach-alignment model: whether an LLM-assisted analysis is appropriate depends on the study’s epistemic values, especially reflexivity, rather than on automation efficiency alone.
- [Artificial intelligence in scientific writing: opportunities and ethical considerations](https://doi.org/10.18203/2320-6012.ijrms20244167): The review treats AI-assisted scientific writing as a pipeline from connecting prior studies and finding gaps to generating evidence and drafting papers, while stressing that prompt-driven efficiency cannot replace human creativity, critical reasoning, compliance, and citation checking.
- [A survey on large language models driven meta-optimizers for automated intelligent optimization](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10462-025-11470-w):The survey defines LLM-driven meta-optimization as a unified paradigm and organizes methods around automatic algorithm generation, dynamic algorithm selection, parameter configuration, and mutation control. It also consolidates evaluation metrics and benchmark problems, then identifies cross-domain generalization, reliability, and automated algorithm-design challenges.
- [An Overview of Challenges, Experiments, and Computational Solutions in Peer Review (Extended Version)](http://www.cs.cmu.edu/~nihars/preprints/SurveyPeerReview.pdf): Surveys peer review as a distributed human-evaluation system, organizing evidence and computational remedies around reviewer assignment, strategic behavior, calibration, subjectivity, identity bias, incentives, and policy while highlighting the field's central evaluation obstacles: no agreed ground truth and limited access to sensitive review data.
- [Evaluation Criteria for Synthetic Respondents in Qualitative Research: Limits of Applicability](https://doi.org/10.19181/inter.2026.18.2.5): This review defines and critiques evaluation criteria for LLM-generated synthetic qualitative respondents, including contextual specificity, contradictions, and interpretive value.
- [What Can Natural Language Processing Do for Peer Review?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06563): Maps peer-review support into concrete NLP tasks such as reviewer assignment, review-quality analysis, paper assessment, and meta-review assistance.
- [Doing Thematic Analysis in the Age of Generative AI: Practices, Ethics and Reflexivity](https://doi.org/10.1177/16094069261425173): The paper maps ChatGPT onto all six phases of reflexive thematic analysis, from transcription and coding to interpretation and proofreading, while requiring researchers to retain reflexive and ethical control.
- [AI for Auto-Research: Roadmap & User Guide](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18661) ([project page](https://worldbench.github.io/awesome-ai-auto-research); [open-source repository](https://github.com/worldbench/awesome-ai-auto-research)) is a curated roadmap and inventory for automated-research agents, benchmarks, skills, and tools; treat it as a discovery resource rather than a standalone benchmark or runnable skill.
- [Claw4Science](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.03.30.715118): Curates the OpenClaw scientific-agent ecosystem into a dataset and platform of projects and skills, helping research agents discover reusable scientific workflows and skill repositories.
- [Exploring multi-view symbolic regression methods in physical sciences.](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.2024.0592):The review compares multi-view symbolic regression in Operon, PySR, phi-SO, and eggp across real-world physical-science datasets. It shows that the implementations can fit shared phenomena with compact parameterized functions, then identifies implementation features associated with better models and gives concrete guidance for future scientific-discovery systems.
- [Large Language Models Penetration in Scholarly Writing and Peer Review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11193): Reviews how LLM use is entering scholarly writing and peer review, including workflow benefits and integrity risks.
- [Agents4Science AI authors and reviewers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15534): Reports lessons from Agents4Science, a conference where AI agents acted as primary authors and reviewers with humans as co-authors and co-reviewers, framing capability and governance questions for human-AI scientific collaboration.
- [Position: Correct Answer, Wrong Mechanism -- When AI Scientists Defend General Claims Their Own Data Contradicts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23175): Position paper argues AI-scientist evaluation must check mechanism fidelity and epistemic honesty, not just final outcome correctness.
- [Detecting Hallucinated and Suspicious Citations: What Current Tools Can and Cannot Do](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22693): Reviews citation-hallucination detection tools and identifies extraction, metadata, coverage, and consistency limits in scholarly source auditing.
- [Science acceleration and accessibility with self-driving labs](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-59231-1): Reviews how self-driving laboratories combine automation, AI, and experimental infrastructure to accelerate discovery and broaden access.
- [Towards Agentic AI for Science: Hypothesis Generation, Comprehension, Quantification, and Validation](https://doi.org/10.1145/3701716.3717754): Frames agentic AI for science around hypothesis generation, comprehension, quantification, and validation.
- [AI ‐Assisted Scientific Figures in Scholarly Publishing: A Risk‐Based Taxonomy by Evidentiary Function](https://doi.org/10.1002/leap.2085): Risk-based taxonomy for AI-assisted scientific figures in scholarly publishing, including disclosure, auditability, labeling, and evidentiary safeguards.
- [Comments on “AI and the advent of the cyborg behavioral scientist”](https://doi.org/10.1002/jcpy.1453): Four reviewer teams converge on an “ask, train, check” discipline: elicit capabilities, customize the model with domain documents and examples, then validate robustness; the commentary also flags synthetic data, audio/visual stimuli, and group-behavior analysis as emerging research uses.
- [AI4Qual: A Comprehensive Field Guide to LLM-Supported Qualitative Research (Tutorial)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3742414.3794947): AI4Qual presents an end-to-end workflow for LLM-assisted qualitative research: consent-aware prompting supports semi-structured interview follow-up, while analysis moves from open codes to themes with human-LLM reliability checks and a RAG path linking findings to source excerpts.
- [A Vision for Auto Research with LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18765): Frames Auto Research as a modular multi-agent lifecycle covering literature review, ideation, methodology planning, experimentation, paper writing, peer-review response, and dissemination, with preliminary feasibility evidence.
- [An AI-powered research assistant in the lab: A practical guide for text analysis through iterative collaboration with LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09724): Presents iterative human-LLM collaboration for lab text analysis, emphasizing prompt refinement, researcher validation, and repeated interpretation rather than unattended qualitative coding.
- [Agentic publications: redesigning scientific publishing in the age of thinking large language models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13246): Proposes "agentic publication" as an interactive knowledge-system layer over papers, combining knowledge graphs, metadata, retrieval, multi-agent verification, APIs, and continuously updated synthesis.
- [A Grassroots Network and Community Roadmap for Interconnected Autonomous Science Laboratories for Accelerated Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17510): Defines AISLE as an interconnected autonomous-lab ecosystem spanning cross-institution equipment orchestration, FAIR data management, agent interfaces, scientific grounding, and AI-integrated education.
- [Position on LLM-Assisted Peer Review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.09182): Argues that LLMs should primarily mentor and give feedback to human reviewers rather than replace them, framing peer-review assistance around reviewer training, feedback quality, and sustainability of the reviewer pool.
- [Who Owns Creativity and Who Does the Work? Trade-offs in LLM-Supported Research Ideation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12152): Analyzes ownership, labor, and agency trade-offs in LLM-supported research ideation, giving Research a human-AI creativity governance reference.
- [Scientific Knowledge Graph Construction Needs an AI-Mediated, Scientist-in-the-Loop Workflow (A Blue Sky Paper)](https://doi.org/10.1109/escience65000.2025.00085): Argues for AI-mediated knowledge-graph construction with scientist review in the loop, positioning entity extraction, relation validation, and domain correction as shared human-AI steps.
- [Agentic AI Scientists Are Not Built For Autonomous Scientific Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08956): A position paper that separates today's co-scientist behavior from genuine autonomous discovery, identifying bottlenecks around hypothesis formation, experiment grounding, evidence integration, and scientific accountability.
- [AI Scientists as Engines of Discovery: A Case for Development within Reformed Institutions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22859): Position work on developing AI scientists within reformed research institutions, covering discovery roles, governance, and evaluation beyond individual tools.
- [A Vocabulary for Multi-Agent Automated Research Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22682): Defines a vocabulary for comparing automated-research systems by roles, operations, state, control, communication, and evaluation.
- [Towards a new paradigm of scientific discovery with socialized artificial intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02775): The position paper argues that scientific discovery needs socialized AI: systems that organize distributed knowledge, roles, and feedback rather than treating autonomous discovery as isolated model inference.
- [Position: AI Agents in Scientific Teams Should Be Studied as Human-Agent Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14667):The paper reframes the unit of analysis for AI Scientists as the human-agent pair, combines literature with real-world case studies, and argues that future work needs mathematical accounts of human-AI synergy and risks such as reduced diversity of inquiry.
- [Data Citation for Large Language Models: A Challenge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25663): The paper frames data citation for LLMs as three open tasks: turning training-data influence into corpus references, citing inference-time datasets or subsets at the right granularity and fixity, and defining what a citation to an individual knowledge-graph triple denotes and how scholarly credit should propagate through provenance.
- [Antipatterns in AI-assisted Qualitative Data Analysis: A Catalog of Temptations and Pitfalls for Software Engineering Researchers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27927): The catalog groups seemingly helpful but rigor-undermining uses of AI-assisted qualitative analysis into Dangerous Drivers, Operational Missteps, and Analytical Failures, giving researchers and reviewers a shared vocabulary for identifying invalid human-AI analysis practices.
- [Scientific Machine Learning (SciML): A Teleological Taxonomy for Decision Support](https://doi.org/10.5220/0015075900004094): The paper replaces driver- and bias-centered SciML taxonomies with a goal-first decision tree: inverse discovery, forward simulation, control and design, and domain adaptation. Its distinctive research-automation contribution is to map a scientist's intended outcome to candidate architectures, explicitly positioning the taxonomy as a foundation for automated SciML model selection.
- [Navigating Artificial Intelligence in Scientific Manuscript Writing: Tips and Traps](https://doi.org/10.1055/s-0044-1800801): This practical review maps how AI can assist literature reviews, statistical analysis, and manuscript language work, while identifying hallucination, bias, loss of authorial judgment, and publisher-policy risks; its distinctive contribution is a human-controlled operating guide for using AI across several research-production steps rather than a claim that drafting can be fully delegated.
- [LLM-based Simulations of Human Behavior in Psychological Research](https://doi.org/10.1609/aies.v8i1.36603): This conceptual review frames LLMs as possible replacements for human participants through the lenses of scientific representation and epistemic opacity, then maps trustful and distrustful positions to methodological responses such as prompt design, fine-tuning, interpretability, and computational cognitive modeling. Its taxonomy clarifies how synthetic-participant protocols should be validated before they are used as a research-execution substitute.
