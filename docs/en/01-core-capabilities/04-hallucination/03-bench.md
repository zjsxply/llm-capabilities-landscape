# 1.4.3 Bench

- [FEVER](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05355): evaluates evidence-based fact checking and claim verification. The core idea is to organize claims, Wikipedia evidence sentences, and supports/refutes/not-enough-info labels into a retrievable and decidable factuality task; it is also one of the early signals aggregated by Hallucinations Leaderboard.
- [TruthfulQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.07958) (dataset: [truthfulqa/truthful_qa](https://huggingface.co/datasets/truthfulqa/truthful_qa)): evaluates whether models answer "truthfully rather than sycophantically" on common misconception questions. The core idea is to design questions where the most common wrong answer is tempting, exposing hallucination and fabrication tendencies.
- [HaluEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11747): evaluates LLMs' ability to recognize and generate hallucinations. The core idea is to construct large-scale hallucinated samples through sampling and filtering, then combine human annotation to check whether models can detect content that conflicts with sources or factual knowledge.
- [FActScore](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251) ([open-source code](https://github.com/shmsw25/FActScore)): evaluates the atomic factual correctness of long-form answers. The core idea is `atomic fact decomposition -> item-level judgment -> aggregate scoring`, separating "verifiability" from subjective evaluation.
- [FELM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00741): evaluates factuality evaluators themselves. The core idea is to annotate LLM outputs with fine-grained, segment-level factual labels and cover many error types including world knowledge, math, and reasoning, rather than only encyclopedic facts.
- [RAGTruth](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00396) ([open-source code](https://github.com/ParticleMedia/RAGTruth)): evaluates word-level hallucination in RAG scenarios. The core idea is to collect nearly 18,000 RAG-generated answers with human annotations, distinguish unsupported and contradictory claims, and specifically support RAG hallucination detection and mitigation.
- [The Dawn After the Dark: An Empirical Study on Factuality Hallucination in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03205): evaluates factuality hallucination across knowledge-intensive QA and generation settings. The core idea is to separate knowledge conflicts, entity errors, and answerability behavior so hallucination is measured as concrete factual mismatch rather than a single refusal or accuracy score.
- [Propagation and Pitfalls: Reasoning-based Assessment of Knowledge Editing through Counterfactual Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.17585): evaluates whether knowledge edits propagate through counterfactual reasoning chains. The protocol checks edited facts, dependent facts, and unrelated facts separately, exposing hallucination-like inconsistencies after editing instead of only testing the edited triple.
- [Merging Facts, Crafting Fallacies: Evaluating the Contradictory Nature of Aggregated Factual Claims in Long-Form Generations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05629): evaluates contradictions created when long-form generations aggregate multiple factual claims. The protocol decomposes generated text into claims and tests whether combinations of individually plausible statements create unsupported or mutually inconsistent conclusions.
- [Self-Alignment for Factuality: Mitigating Hallucinations in LLMs via Self-Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09267): defer or move to Model: the contribution is a self-evaluation and revision method for factuality, with benchmark results as evidence rather than a standalone reusable benchmark.
- [HGOT: Hierarchical Graph of Thoughts for Retrieval-Augmented In-Context Learning in Factuality Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09390): defer or move to Agent Harness or Model: the paper proposes a hierarchical graph-of-thoughts factuality evaluator for retrieval-augmented in-context learning rather than releasing a primary hallucination benchmark.
- [Do LLMs Know about Hallucination? An Empirical Investigation of LLM's Hidden States](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09733): evaluates whether internal hidden-state signals distinguish hallucinated and factual generations. The diagnostic protocol compares layerwise representations and probing signals against hallucination labels, making model self-knowledge about factuality the measured phenomenon.
- [Comparing Hallucination Detection Metrics for Multilingual Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10496): evaluates hallucination detection metrics for multilingual generation. The study compares metric behavior across languages and generation tasks, highlighting where English-centric factuality or faithfulness scores fail under multilingual outputs.
- [PEDANTS: Cheap but Effective and Interpretable Answer Equivalence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11161): evaluates answer equivalence as an interpretable metric for open-ended QA grading. The method compares candidate and reference answers through inexpensive, explainable equivalence checks, useful when hallucination scoring depends on whether two surface-different answers state the same fact.
- [Benchmarking Knowledge Boundary for Large Language Model: A Different Perspective on Model Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11493): evaluates whether models know when queried facts fall inside or outside their knowledge boundary. The benchmark stresses answerability and uncertainty, making unsupported confident answers a measurable hallucination mode.
- [Exploring the Impact of Table-to-Text Methods on Augmenting LLM-based Question Answering with Domain Hybrid Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12869): defer or move to Model or data-generation notes: the main contribution studies table-to-text augmentation for domain QA rather than defining a hallucination benchmark.
- [TofuEval: Evaluating Hallucinations of LLMs on Topic-Focused Dialogue Summarization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13249): evaluates hallucinations in topic-focused dialogue summarization. The dataset and protocol compare summaries against dialogue evidence for topic relevance and faithfulness, targeting dialogue-specific omissions and unsupported additions.
- [Beyond Probabilities: Unveiling the Misalignment in Evaluating Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13887): evaluates mismatch between probability-based scores and actual LLM correctness. The diagnostic setup tests whether likelihood or confidence signals align with factual answer quality, exposing reliability failures hidden by token probabilities.
- [MeTMaP: Metamorphic Testing for Detecting False Vector Matching Problems in LLM Augmented Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14480): evaluates false vector matching in LLM-augmented generation through metamorphic testing. The protocol perturbs retrieval or vector-matching conditions and checks whether generated answers remain grounded when nearest-neighbor evidence is misleading.
- [AttributionBench: How Hard is Automatic Attribution Evaluation?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15089): evaluates automatic attribution metrics for generated text. The benchmark tests whether metrics can judge claim-to-source support under controlled attribution errors, distinguishing citation presence from actual evidence support.
- [Hal-Eval: A Universal and Fine-grained Hallucination Evaluation Framework for Large Vision Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15721): evaluates fine-grained visual hallucination in LVLMs. The framework organizes object, attribute, relation, and scene-level hallucination checks so multimodal factuality errors are scored beyond coarse answer accuracy.
- [HypoTermQA: Hypothetical Terms Dataset for Benchmarking Hallucination Tendency of LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16211): evaluates hallucination tendency on invented or hypothetical terms. The dataset asks about non-existent concepts, making fabricated definitions and overconfident explanations directly observable.
- [DiaHalu: A Dialogue-level Hallucination Evaluation Benchmark for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00896): evaluates hallucination at the dialogue level rather than isolated turns. The benchmark checks whether responses remain consistent with conversation history, external facts, and dialogue context across multi-turn interactions.
- [FENICE: Factuality Evaluation of summarization based on Natural language Inference and Claim Extraction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02270): evaluates summarization factuality with claim extraction and natural-language inference. The metric decomposes summaries into checkable claims and scores support against source documents, targeting unsupported summary content.
- [Benchmarking Hallucination in Large Language Models Based on Unanswerable Math Word Problem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03558): evaluates hallucination on unanswerable math word problems. The benchmark uses underspecified or impossible math questions to test whether models abstain or fabricate numerical solutions.
- [ERBench: An Entity-Relationship based Automatically Verifiable Hallucination Benchmark for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05266): evaluates entity-relation hallucinations with automatically verifiable questions. The benchmark constructs checks around entity relationships so answers can be validated programmatically rather than by broad subjective judgment.
- [PhD: A ChatGPT-Prompted Visual hallucination Evaluation Dataset](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11116): evaluates visual hallucination in image-question answering using ChatGPT-prompted data construction. The dataset targets cases where models mention absent objects, incorrect attributes, or unsupported visual details.
- [WikiFactDiff: A Large, Realistic, and Temporally Adaptable Dataset for Atomic Factual Knowledge Update in Causal Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14364): evaluates atomic factual knowledge updates with temporally adaptable Wikipedia-derived facts. The dataset supports before-and-after checks for whether models preserve, update, or hallucinate facts as knowledge changes.
- [Pensieve: Retrospect-then-Compare Mitigates Visual Hallucination](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14401): defer or move to Model: the contribution is a retrospect-then-compare mitigation method for visual hallucination, not a standalone benchmark artifact.
- [LongFact](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18802) ([open-source code](https://github.com/google-deepmind/long-form-factuality)): evaluates factuality and evidence support in long-form generation. The core idea is to split answers into verifiable atomic claims and use a search-augmented judgment protocol to measure overall factuality; the paper also includes settings such as LongFact-Objects/Concepts.
- [Multi-hop Question Answering under Temporal Knowledge Editing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00492): evaluates multi-hop QA after temporal knowledge edits. The protocol tests whether an edited time-sensitive fact propagates through dependent reasoning chains without causing stale or contradictory answers.
- [Evaluating the Factuality of Large Language Models using Large-Scale Knowledge Graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00942): evaluates LLM factuality against large-scale knowledge graphs. The protocol converts graph facts into queries and checks generated answers against structured triples, emphasizing entity-relation correctness at scale.
- [FABLES: Evaluating faithfulness and content selection in book-length summarization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01261): Benchmarks hallucination capabilities with a task, evaluation protocol, or diagnostic setting.
- [The Hallucinations Leaderboard - An Open Effort to Measure Hallucinations in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05904) ([leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/spaces/hallucinations-leaderboard/leaderboard)): aggregates multiple hallucination and factuality signals into a public leaderboard. The protocol makes model comparisons reproducible across factual QA, summarization, and grounded generation rather than tying the ranking to one dataset.
- [Constructing Benchmarks and Interventions for Combating Hallucinations in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09971): combines hallucination benchmark construction with mitigation experiments. As a Bench entry, its reusable part is the curated evaluation cases for detecting unsupported or false model outputs; the intervention component should be treated as Model-side evidence.

The core idea is to aggregate multi-source factual-consistency data and compare detectors under one framework for judging whether answers are supported by reference documents.
- [VALOR-EVAL: Holistic Coverage and Faithfulness Evaluation of Large Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13874): evaluates LVLM descriptions along two axes: visual coverage of salient image content and faithfulness to objects, attributes, and relations. - [Evaluating Consistency and Reasoning Capabilities of Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16478): evaluates factual consistency under reasoning-style prompts and answer variants.
