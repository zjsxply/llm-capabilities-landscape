# 1.17.2 Survey

- [Algorithmic Creativity: A Systematic Review of AI and Artistic Expression in the Digital African Landscape](https://doi.org/10.58721/pajmae.v3i1.1518): Systematically reviews AI and artistic expression in the African creative landscape using major databases and grey literature from 2014 to 2025 with thematic synthesis.
- [Envisioning the Future of Creative Thinking Assessment](https://doi.org/10.1002/jocb.70036): Reflects on PISA 2022 creative-thinking assessment and argues for scalable scoring methods that preserve multidomain, multilingual creative-thinking coverage.
- [On the Creativity of Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00008): Reviews LLM creativity through classic creativity theory. Core idea: organize the discussion around value, novelty, and surprise, then separate product, process, press, and person perspectives so "creative writing" demonstrations are not mistaken for settled evidence of machine creativity.
- [Open-Endedness is Essential for Artificial Superhuman Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04268): Positions open-endedness as a prerequisite for superhuman AI. Core idea: define open-endedness through novelty and learnability, then argue that foundation-model systems need self-improving discovery loops rather than fixed benchmark optimization.
- [A Survey on Compositional Learning of AI Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08787): Surveys compositional learning theory, tasks, evaluation benchmarks, and model families. Core idea: gives the creativity page a technical baseline for separating ordinary compositional generalization from the more open-ended combinatorial creativity targeted by later benchmarks.
- [Creativity in AI: Progresses and Challenges](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17218): Surveys progress and challenges in AI creativity, organizing current generative capabilities, evaluation limits, and open problems around machine creativity.
- [AI-Augmented Design Thinking: Potentials, Challenges, and Mitigation Strategies of Integrating Artificial Intelligence in Human-Centered Innovation Processes](https://doi.org/10.1109/emr.2024.3512866): Uses affordance analysis of professional design-thinking workshops to identify AI potentials, challenges, and mitigation strategies for human-centered innovation processes.
- [Advances in artificial intelligence: a review for the creative industries](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02725): Reviews AI advances for creative industries, organizing how generative systems affect creative workflows, production roles, and sector-specific adoption.
- [The Theater Stage as Laboratory: Review of Real-Time Comedy LLM Systems for Live Performance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08474): Reviews real-time comedy LLM systems for live performance and argues that AI comedy should be evaluated with audiences under live physical or online conditions.
- [Generative AI and Creative Work: Narratives, Values, and Impacts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03940): Reviews online-media narratives around GenAI creative work, identifying automation and productivity frames that detach ideas from labor, material execution, and craft skills.
- [XAIxArts Manifesto: Explainable AI for the Arts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.21220): Repositions explainable AI for the arts away from technocentric productivity explanations and toward artist-facing values, materials, and creative interpretation.
- [De-skilling, Cognitive Offloading, and Misplaced Responsibilities: Potential Ironies of AI-Assisted Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03924): Analyzes more than 120 UX and design articles plus practitioner perceptions to map de-skilling, cognitive offloading, and responsibility risks in AI-assisted design.
- [Human and Machine as Seen at the Co-Creation Age: A Co-Word Analysis in Human Machine Co-creation (2014-2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.14363): Uses co-word analysis over CHI human-machine co-creation papers from 2014 to 2024 to map the shift from tool use toward collaborative creative partnership.
- [A Systematic Review of Human-AI Co-Creativity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21333): Reviews 62 co-creative systems across application domains, interaction patterns, human roles, AI roles, and evaluation practices for human-AI creativity support.
- [Music and Artificial Intelligence: Artistic Trends](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11694): Surveys 337 AI music artworks and categorizes co-composition, sound design, lyrics, translation, performance, and installation trends.
- [The Variance Paradox: How AI Reduces Diversity but Increases Novelty](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19264): The Variance Paradox frames AI creativity through variance compression, epistemic deference, and cross-domain recombination, predicting when diversity loss can later enable novelty.
- [Generative AI in Game Development: A Qualitative Research Synthesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11898): Synthesizes qualitative research on how GenAI is reshaping game-development pipelines, production roles, and value networks.
- [Combinatorial Creativity: A New Frontier in Generalization Abilities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21043): Defines combinatorial creativity as open-ended generalization distinct from compositional generalization, using novelty-utility scoring and an algorithmic task to study creativity scaling, depth-width tradeoffs, and the novelty-utility source of the ideation-execution gap.
- [MetricalARGS: A Taxonomy for Studying Metrical Poetry with LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08188): MetricalARGS gives a taxonomy for LLM work on metrical poetry, organizing rhyme, rhythm, genre, and structure as analyzable creative-writing dimensions.
- [Large Language Models for Scientific Idea Generation: A Creativity-Centered Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.07448): This survey organizes LLM-based scientific ideation into external knowledge augmentation, prompt-level steering, inference-time scaling, multi-agent collaboration, and parameter adaptation, then interprets novelty with Boden's taxonomy and Rhodes's four-P framework while separating novelty from scientific validity.
- [Artificial Creativity: from predictive AI to Generative System 3](https://doi.org/10.3389/frai.2025.1654716): Frames artificial creativity as a move from predictive AI toward Generative System 3, emphasizing metacognitive and generative control beyond output imitation.
- [Creative Planning with Language Models: Practice, Evaluation and Applications](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/2025.naacl-tutorial.1): Tutorial on creative planning with language models, connecting planning methods, evaluation protocols, and application patterns for open-ended generation.
- [Disembodied creativity in generative AI: prima facie challenges and limitations of prompting in creative practice](https://doi.org/10.3389/frai.2025.1651354): Analyzes prompting-centered GenAI through tacit expertise and embodied practice, clarifying why creative work cannot be reduced to natural-language instructions.
- [The study of human-AI Co-creation design under generative artificial intelligence: cognition, process, method, and outcome](https://doi.org/10.1080/09544828.2025.2567155): This human-AI co-creation design review organizes generative-AI collaboration around cognition, process, method, outcome, boundary conditions, and future designer-AI fusion models.
- [Creativity and aesthetic evaluation of AI-generated artworks: bridging problems and methods from psychology to AI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1648480): Bridges creativity psychology and empirical aesthetics for AI art, organizing indices for creativity and aesthetic judgment that can guide AI-generated artwork studies.
- [When humans and large language models collaborate, problem-finding illuminates](https://doi.org/10.1080/14479338.2025.2504428): Frames human-LLM problem finding for ill-structured organizational problems around cognitive activities and skills, separating problem formulation from downstream problem solving in AI-assisted innovation.
- [Can AI read the room?: Attuning machines to the affective atmospheres of stand-up comedy performance](https://doi.org/10.1080/2040610x.2025.2544414): Analyzes whether AI can infer the affective atmosphere of live and digital stand-up comedy, using performance theory to define what humor agents miss when they only process text.
- [GenAI and Creative Labor: New Evidence on Valuation, Inequality, and Adoption](https://doi.org/10.1177/07308884261450882): Frames GenAI creative labor through valuation, collaboration, inequality, and adoption, adding special-issue evidence and a 2025 SNAAP Pulse survey of creative workers.
- [Acquisition of Intelligent Design Knowledge Driven by UGC: Technical Approaches, Challenges, and Prospects](https://doi.org/10.1109/eeiss65394.2025.11086078): Reviews technical approaches for extracting product features and design knowledge from user-generated content, then identifies bottlenecks for intelligent design decision-support systems.
- [Irrationality in humans and creativity in AI](https://doi.org/10.3389/frai.2025.1579704): Connects human irrationality, the conjunction fallacy, and default-mode, executive-control, and salience networks to propose non-linear reasoning signals for AI creativity.
- [An Economics of Creative Machines: The Role and Responsibility of Human-AI Creators](https://doi.org/10.1109/r10-htc63995.2025.11394507): Models human-AI creative production with a Cobb-Douglas framework, cost minimization, and Rényi-entropy knowledge integration, giving creativity research an economic lens on when AI shifts from generator to tool.
- [Artificial Creativity? AI’s Short- and Long-Term Impact on Creativity](https://doi.org/10.1080/08956308.2025.2450756): Reviews how generative AI changes creative thinking in the short and long term, linking practitioner adoption, science-policy questions, and creativity research rather than treating AI art as isolated output quality.
- [AI & arts: people-centred practice](https://doi.org/10.1080/14702029.2025.2578870): Introduces a people-centred AI and arts special issue, organizing creative AI practice around co-design, community participation, institutional reflection, listening, access, and co-creation rather than treating AI art as detached automation.
- [DIGITAL AESTHETICS IN THE AGE OF MACHINE LEARNING](https://doi.org/10.29121/shodhkosh.v6.i5s.2025.6892): Reviews how image synthesis, style transfer, aesthetic scoring, interpretability, and cultural alignment reshape digital-art generation, evaluation, and perception in machine-learning-based creative systems.
- [The Role of AI in Democratizing Visual Storytelling](https://doi.org/10.29121/shodhkosh.v6.i4s.2025.6860): Reviews how generative image, style-transfer, multimodal storytelling, and automated editing systems lower barriers to visual storytelling.
- [Reinventing Digital Illustration with Generative AI Tools](https://doi.org/10.29121/shodhkosh.v6.i5s.2025.6876): Frames digital illustration as interaction between human intent and GAN, VAE, and diffusion inference, with similarity evaluation between AI and human artwork.
- [A Systematic Review of Generative AI on Game Character Creation: Applications, Challenges, and Future Trends](https://doi.org/10.1109/TG.2025.3564869): Reviews generative-AI pipelines for game character creation, organizing applications, production challenges, and future trends for character assets.
- [The Bright Side of the Moon: Transfer Learning and Creativity in Machine Learning Practice](https://doi.org/10.1080/10400419.2025.2565357): Connects transfer learning with creativity in machine-learning practice, treating cross-domain reuse and adaptation as part of how creative ML systems and practitioners generate new solutions.
- [Creative Thinking Assessment Across Cultures: Challenges and Considerations](https://doi.org/10.1002/jocb.70025): Examines how language, cultural values, and standardized PISA assumptions can distort cross-cultural creative-thinking assessment, and argues for flexible multidimensional frameworks that preserve diverse forms of creativity.
- [Group Selection as a Safeguard Against AI Substitution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.03541): Uses agent-based modeling and evolutionary game theory to compare AI as creative complement versus substitute, explaining when homogenized AI use can cause cultural-collapse dynamics.
- [Beyond Input–Output: Rethinking Creativity through Design-by-Analogy in Human–AI Collaboration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09423): Reframes human-AI creativity through design-by-analogy, arguing that evaluation should track analogical search and transformation processes rather than only final outputs.
- [Design Generative AI for Practitioners: Exploring Interaction Approaches Aligned with Creative Practice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03074): Synthesizes DesignPrompt, FusAIn, and DesignTrace into an interaction taxonomy where designers steer generative AI through intent scaffolds, direct visual input, and process traces.
- [Beyond Semantic Similarity: Open Challenges for Embedding-Based Creative Process Analysis Across AI Design Tools](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07611): Beyond Semantic Similarity critiques fixed embedding similarity for creative process analysis and organizes challenges around creative significance, multimodal trace segmentation, and agentic systems whose metrics affect generation.
- [Exploring Multimodal Generative Systems: Efficient Training and Evaluation for Visual and Creative Applications](https://doi.org/10.29121/shodhkosh.v7.i2s.2026.7265): Surveys multimodal generative-system training and evaluation for visual creative applications, linking efficiency choices with image and design output assessment.
- [Designing for Meaning in AIGC Systems: The Control–Ownership Pathway in Human–AI Co-Creation](https://doi.org/10.1109/access.2026.3679569): Uses a 299-user AIGC study with partial least squares structural equation modeling and fuzzy-set qualitative comparative analysis to link interactivity, control, ownership, and perceived meaning in co-creation.
- [The use of artificial intelligence in new product development: A systematic literature review, conceptual framework, and future research agenda](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.technovation.2026.103541): Systematically reviews artificial intelligence in new product development and organizes the literature into a conceptual framework and future research agenda for AI-supported product ideation and design.
- [Semantic Preservation in LLM-Based Cultural Narrative Generation for Marketing Communication Contexts](https://doi.org/10.1109/icnlp69856.2026.11528070): Analyzes semantic preservation in LLM-based cultural narrative generation, identifying structural compression, salience redistribution, pragmatic drift, and symbolic attenuation as recurring failure modes.
- [Artificial intelligence across design thinking: a qualitative review](https://doi.org/10.1017/dsj.2026.10054): Reviews 205 Web of Science studies with the SPAR-4-SLR protocol, mapping how AI changes design-thinking stages from evidence gathering and ideation to prototyping, testing, and risks such as bias, opacity, and creative homogenization.
- [Embodied Intelligence and Algorithmic Expression](https://doi.org/10.29121/shodhkosh.v7.i1.2026.7603): Builds a case corpus of fifteen AI-dance systems and compares their inputs, outputs, interaction loops, embodiment, and agency sharing.
- [Towards Personalizing Generative AI with Small Data for Co-Creation in the Visual Arts](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3660/paper11.pdf): Frames artist-led small-data personalization as a co-creation path, contrasting direct model adaptation with prompt-only navigation of generative spaces.
- [Generative artificial intelligence in creative contexts](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11301-025-00494-9): Systematically reviews GenAI research in creative contexts. Core idea: synthesize 64 studies into adoption factors, creative-process effects, industry impacts, and future questions about long-term practice change, socio-economic consequences, and ethical use.
- [Perceptions and integration of generative artificial intelligence in creative practices and industries](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-025-02667-2): Maps how creative professionals adopt GenAI across visual art, writing, performing arts, and spatial design. Core idea: use a scoping review and conceptual model to show a shift from creation to curation, with adoption shaped by tacit knowledge, materiality, career stage, and fear of homogenization.
- [AI-driven design: a comprehensive review of its methodologies, applications, and challenges](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00158-026-04331-9): Surveys AI-driven design as a creativity-adjacent engineering field. Core idea: collect methods, applications, and challenge axes for AI-assisted design so product and engineering ideation work can be distinguished from generic optimization applications.
- [Creativity in AI as Emergence from Domain-Limited Generative Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.08388): Reframes AI creativity as a generative-mechanism problem rather than only post-hoc output scoring. Core idea: explain creative emergence in domain-limited generative models through pattern-based generation, induced world models, contextual grounding, and arbitrarity.
- [AI ethics in creative domains](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-026-01044-z): Reviews AI ethics across creative-domain detection, recognition, interpretation, and generation. Core idea: treat art and creative production as a capability setting with distinct authorship, attribution, authenticity, and moral-evaluation risks rather than a generic AI-ethics appendix.
- [A Taxonomy of Human–MLLM Interaction in Early-Stage Sketch-Based Design Ideation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22171): Taxonomizes human-MLLM interaction patterns in early sketch-based design ideation, mapping how multimodal models participate in creative design workflows.
- [Assessing the Creativity of Large Language Models: Testing, Limits, and New Frontiers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13450): Tests whether human creativity instruments predict LLM creative achievement across creative writing, divergent thinking, and scientific ideation; finds DAT and Conditional DAT are construct-specific predictors and introduces DRAT as a robust predictor of scientific ideation.
- [Designing Interactions with Generative AI for Art and Creativity](https://doi.org/10.1145/3715336.3735843): Systematically reviews GenAI interaction designs for art and creativity, organizing tools, user roles, and design patterns into a taxonomy for creative co-production.
- [Exploring Collaboration Patterns and Strategies in Human-AI Co-creation through the Lens of Agency](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.06000): Reviews top-tier HCI work through an agency lens, mapping how initiative, control, and responsibility move between humans and AI during co-creation.
- [Human-AI Co-Creativity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12527): Synthesizes levels of creative collaboration between humans and AI, clarifying when systems act as tools, partners, critics, or co-authors.
- [Human-Centered AI Communication in Co-Creativity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18385): Builds a communication framework for co-creativity, foregrounding how feedback, explanation, and initiative shape human-AI creative collaboration.
- [Narrative Theory-Driven LLM Methods for Automatic Story Generation and Understanding: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15851): This survey organizes LLM story generation and understanding through narrative theory, giving the creativity page a structured view of plot, character, discourse, and evaluation choices for automatic storytelling.
- [“Control Is a Trajectory, Not a Point”: Conceptualizing Control in Human-AI Co-Creativity](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3790861): This work conceptualizes control in human-AI co-creativity as a trajectory across the creative process, giving interaction designers a vocabulary for when users and AI systems should shift initiative.
- [Generative Artificial Intelligence and the Creative Industries: A Bibliometric Review and Research Agenda](https://doi.org/10.3390/systems14020138): Maps GenAI in creative industries with PRISMA screening, bibliometric analysis, and thematic clusters covering labor, platforms, governance, workflows, and creative production.
- [Human-AI Interaction Patterns in Creative Domains and Their Time-Based Visualization](https://doi.org/10.1145/3743049.3743081): Taxonomizes time-based human-AI interaction patterns in creative domains, giving co-creation systems a workflow vocabulary for initiative and iteration.
- [Ideation with Generative AI—in Consumer Research and Beyond](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucaf012): Frames generative AI as an ideation partner across roles such as designer, writer, interviewer, and actor, clarifying where LLMs support creative research workflows.
- [The Impact of Generative AI on Creativity in Software Development: A Research Agenda](https://doi.org/10.1145/3708523): This research agenda maps GenAI's effects on software-development creativity across individual capabilities, team capabilities, products, unintended consequences, and society.
- [The Role of Artificial Intelligence in the Ideation Process](https://doi.org/10.1111/jpim.12791): This review organizes AI in ideation into opportunity analysis, idea generation, and idea screening or selection, highlighting where AI improves average creativity and where selection remains weak.
- [AI Visual Art History: An Art Movement with Expanded Artistic Horizon](https://doi.org/10.1145/3726868): AI Visual Art History traces AI visual art as an emerging art movement, relating AI art to conventional art history, artist-computer symbiosis, evaluation criteria, and democratization of visual creation.
- [Has AI Surpassed Humans in Creative Idea Generation? A Meta-Analysis](https://doi.org/10.1145/3746175.3746189): Meta-analyzes 17 studies and 115 effect sizes on human versus GenAI creative ideation, finding no robust evidence that GenAI has surpassed humans overall.
- [Incorporating Personality into AI Writing Companions: Mapping the Design Space](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706599.3720185): Maps AI writing companion functions, personality traits, and visual design elements for human engagement.
- [Generative AI as a probabilistic medium: a systems-theoretical analysis of the art system](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-026-03111-9): Uses a systems-theoretical lens to frame GenAI as a probabilistic medium in the art system, clarifying how machine-generated variation changes artistic production and evaluation.
- [Human‐AI Co‐Creativity: Does ChatGPT Make Us More Creative?](https://doi.org/10.1002/jocb.70022): Reframes ChatGPT creativity evidence around human-AI co-creativity, separating standalone model creativity claims from interaction effects on human creative outcomes.
- [AI‐Augmented Approaches to Creative Problem‐Solving: A Metacognitive Perspective](https://doi.org/10.1111/caim.70003): Frames GenAI in creative problem solving through metacognition, distinguishing tool-like assistance from actor-like roles across individual CPS processes.
- [Peeping at creAItivity through a keyhole: creative self-perceptions, potential, and enhancement of GenAI chatbots](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10462-025-11288-6): Reviews GenAI chatbot creativity through self-perception, creative potential, and enhancement strategies, giving chatbot creativity a structured assessment lens.
- [Algorithms in the Realm of Fuzziness. How Generative Artificial Intelligence Is Changing the Front End of Innovation](https://doi.org/10.1111/caim.70048): Studies how GenAI reshapes the front end of innovation, especially opportunity identification and ideation activities that precede formal product development.
- [AI as a Helper: Leveraging Generative AI Tools Across Common Parts of the Creative Process](https://doi.org/10.3390/jintelligence13050057): Maps GenAI support across problem identification, idea generation, idea evaluation, and deployment, giving the creative process a staged AI-assistance framework.
- [Cyber-Creativity: A Decalogue of Research Challenges](https://doi.org/10.3390/jintelligence13080103): Identifies ten research challenges for human-AI creativity, organizing benefits and risks of AI within creative processes rather than treating creativity as a single output score.
- [From friction to synergy: The complex interplay of human creativity and AI](https://doi.org/10.1177/27538699251350483): Analyzes human-AI creativity through affective, embodied, experiential, and sociocultural dimensions, arguing that productive friction should be monitored rather than erased in creative support systems.
- [Creativity across domains](https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgaf174): Connects creativity in science, engineering, mathematics, computing, technology, and art, using historical cross-domain examples to frame how computational tools and AI may accelerate or reshape creative work.
- [Is Memory the Key to AI Creativity? Exploring the Intersection of Artificial Intelligence and Artistic Expression](https://doi.org/10.1002/jocb.70051): Connects catastrophic forgetting, continual learning, and artistic AI through literature review and creative-technologist interviews, framing memory as a mechanism for sustained creative progression.
- [Organizing across cognitive asymmetry in human–AI collaboration: A study of perfume creation](https://doi.org/10.1002/smj.70089): Studies professional perfume creation to explain human-AI cognitive asymmetry, identifying task allocation, tacit-to-codified knowledge conversion, and output steering as integration practices.
- [“The Cat Sat on the …?” Why Generative AI Has Limited Creativity](https://doi.org/10.1002/jocb.70077): Applies a standard creativity definition ex ante to LLM outputs and argues that autoregressive generation imposes a mathematical upper bound near little-c to Pro-c creativity.
- [AI: An Active and Innovative Tool for Artistic Creation](https://doi.org/10.3390/arts14030052): Traces the historical and conceptual integration of generative AI into art and grounds authorship, legitimacy, and cultural-impact claims in case analyses of Refik Anadol’s *Unsupervised* and Anna Ridler’s *Mosaic Virus*.
- [Generative AI in Game Design: Enhancing Creativity or Constraining Innovation?](https://doi.org/10.3390/jintelligence13060060): Uses a mixed-method study with 42 game-design professionals and 9 follow-up interviews to analyze how GenAI changes ideation, prototyping, decision making, originality, and creative dependency in game design.
- [Augment, not replace: Utilising AI tools for documentary podcast production.](https://doi.org/10.24135/ijcmr.v13iapril.167): Documents a five-episode documentary-podcast production workflow and evaluates AI tools across pre-production, production, and post-production as augmentation of specialist creative judgment.
- [A Bibliometric Analysis and Systematic Review of Artificial Intelligence in Fashion Design](https://doi.org/10.65660/stardergisi.1811605): Uses Scopus queries over artificial intelligence, text-to-image, and fashion design to analyze 97 publications, then reviews the most-cited and recent works to map AI tool types and design-stage use in fashion creativity.
- [Designers as Co-Creators: Reconfiguring Workflows and Creative Agency in AI-Augmented Design](https://doi.org/10.1002/pra2.1528): Interviews 14 professional designers and maps AI use across discovery, ideation, development, refinement, and finalization, using a workflow heatmap to show where AI accelerates iteration and where contextual judgment remains human-led.
- [Generative AI Technology for Safeguarding Intangible Cultural Heritage: A Systematic Review](https://doi.org/10.1145/3785987.3785989): Systematically reviews generative AI for intangible-cultural-heritage safeguarding, comparing GANs, diffusion models, neural style transfer, autoregressive models, and VAEs across digital innovation, generation, and cross-cultural reuse.
- [A Framework for Human-AI Dance Creation: The Machinic Movement Matrix](https://doi.org/10.1145/3802842.3802849): Introduces a three-axis taxonomy of generativity, responsiveness, and agency distribution for analyzing machine roles in choreographic co-creation.
- [The Use of Large Language Models in HCI: A Critical Analysis of Synthetic Users](https://doi.org/10.1145/3745900.3746108): Critiques LLM-generated synthetic users in HCI, mapping adoption pressures, use cases, normative-bias risks, and why they are especially unreliable for usability testing or interviews.
- [The Investigation of Generative AI on Role-Playing Games: Applications, Challenges, and Future Trends](https://doi.org/10.54097/m90bkr64): Reviews AIGC in desktop role-playing games, emphasizing GPT-style dynamic task and narrative generation as a route to less homogeneous game experiences.
- [The Co-Creative Design Framework for Hybrid Intelligence](https://doi.org/10.1145/3698061.3726934): Formalizes human-AI co-creation around agency, interaction dynamics, and communication so generative-AI design systems can be analyzed as hybrid creative collaborations.
- [Designing Co-Creative Systems: Five Paradoxes in Human-AI Collaboration](https://doi.org/10.3390/info16100909): Frames human-AI co-creation around five design tensions: ambiguity versus precision, control versus serendipity, speed versus reflection, individual versus collective, and originality versus remix.
- [A Theoretical Framework of the Structure of Augmented Creativity](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10796-026-10745-z): Builds a sociomaterial framework for augmented creativity where humans and GenAI jointly produce novel and useful outcomes, connecting agency, tools, and creative-process structure.
- [On the Creativity of AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13242): Analyzes LLM-agent creativity through functionalist and ontological perspectives, arguing that agents can exhibit observable creative outputs while still lacking social, personal, and process-level dimensions of creativity.
- [Position: LLMs can’t jump](https://openreview.net/forum?id=klU4737opt):The paper argues that next-token-trained LLMs are strong at interpolating and recombining patterns represented in their training distribution but cannot reliably make the discontinuous conceptual jumps associated with transformative discovery. It frames this limitation as a research agenda for distinguishing plausible recombination from genuinely novel creative reasoning.
