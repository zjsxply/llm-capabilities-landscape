# 1.4.2 Survey

- [Survey of Hallucination in Natural Language Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.03629): A foundational taxonomy of intrinsic and extrinsic hallucination in NLG, covering causes, datasets, detection, and mitigation.
- [A Comprehensive Survey of Hallucination Mitigation Techniques in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01313): Organizes mitigation by retrieval grounding, verification, decoding, prompting, and training-side interventions.
- [The Chronicles of RAG: The Retriever, the Chunk and the Generator](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07883): Treats RAG as a grounding pipeline and reviews retriever choice, chunking, generation quality, and evaluation practices for reducing unsupported answers.
- [The Pitfalls of Defining Hallucination](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07897): Uses data-to-text NLG and logic-based definitions to separate hallucination from omission and clarify where factuality evaluations disagree.
- [Factuality of Large Language Models: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02420): Reframes hallucination under factuality, linking evaluation, benchmark design, correction, and reliability risks.
- [A Survey on Large Language Model Hallucination via a Creativity Perspective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06647): Contrasts reliability harms with creative-use cases by mapping hallucination to divergent and convergent thinking in LLM applications.
- [Retrieval-Augmented Generation for AI-Generated Content: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19473): Surveys RAG architectures for grounding generated content in external evidence and limiting unsupported generation.
- [Knowledge Conflicts for LLMs: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08319): Classifies context-memory, inter-context, and intra-memory conflicts that drive factual errors when parametric and retrieved knowledge disagree.
- [Hallucination Detection in Foundation Models for Decision-Making: A Flexible Definition and Review of the State of the Art](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16527): Extends hallucination detection to decision-making systems by emphasizing out-of-distribution states, planning failures, and safety-critical deployment boundaries.
- [Visual Hallucination: Definition, Quantification, and Prescriptive Remediations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17306): Defines eight VLM visual-hallucination orientations across captioning and VQA, then connects quantification to remediation strategies.
- [An Audit on the Perspectives and Challenges of Hallucinations in NLP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07461): Audits 103 NLP papers and practitioner responses to expose inconsistent definitions, terminology drift, and societal risk perceptions.
- [A Survey of Automatic Hallucination Evaluation on Natural Language Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12041): Systematizes automatic hallucination evaluation with datasets, benchmarks, and 105 methods, highlighting the shift toward LLM-specific evaluators.
- [Hallucination of Multimodal Large Language Models: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18930): Reviews MLLM hallucination causes, identification, evaluation, and mitigation for outputs inconsistent with visual evidence.
- [A Comprehensive Survey of Hallucination in Large Language, Image, Video and Audio Foundation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09589): Compares hallucination manifestations, detection, and mitigation across text, image, video, and audio foundation models.
- [When Can LLMs Actually Correct Their Own Mistakes? A Critical Survey of Self-Correction of LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01297): Separates self-evaluation from external-feedback correction and identifies when self-correction experiments overstate factuality gains.
- [A Taxonomy for Data Contamination in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08716): Classifies pretraining contamination types and decontamination limits that can inflate factuality evaluations and mask hallucination risk.
- [LLMs Will Always Hallucinate, and We Need to Live With This](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.05746): Presents a theoretical position that hallucination is structurally unavoidable, motivating risk management rather than elimination-only framing.
- [Trustworthiness in Retrieval-Augmented Generation Systems: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10102): Reviews RAG trustworthiness across retrieval quality, generation faithfulness, robustness, privacy, and attribution.
- [A Survey of Hallucination in Large Visual Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15359): Surveys LVLM hallucination causes, correction and mitigation methods, datasets, and evaluation metrics.
- [A Survey on Uncertainty Quantification of Large Language Models: Taxonomy, Open Research Challenges, and Future Directions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05563): Treats uncertainty as a hallucination signal and organizes token, sequence, semantic, and model-level quantification methods.
- [Beyond Isolated Fixes: A Comprehensive Survey on Hallucination Mitigation with a Three-Dimensional Taxonomy and Integrative Framework](https://doi.org/10.1109/uv63228.2024.11189154): Uses a three-dimensional taxonomy to connect isolated mitigation techniques into an integrated hallucination-control framework.
- [A Survey of Hallucination Problems Based on Large Language Models](https://doi.org/10.54254/2755-2721/2024.17851): Provides a compact LLM hallucination overview spanning causes, detection, evaluation, and mitigation.
- [A Review of Faithfulness Metrics for Hallucination Assessment in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.00269): Reviews faithfulness metrics across summarization, question answering, and machine translation, including LLM-as-judge and RAG-based mitigation evidence.
- [LLMs as Repositories of Factual Knowledge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12774): Reviews how parametric memory stores and fails to retrieve factual knowledge, complementing hallucination and factuality surveys.
- [A Survey of Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Customized Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13958): Focuses on graph-structured retrieval as a grounding layer for customized LLMs and domain-specific factual consistency.
- [Hallucination, monofacts, and miscalibration: An empirical investigation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08666): Empirically connects hallucination rates to monofacts and calibration, testing a statistical lower-bound view of factual errors.
- [Ask in Any Modality: A Comprehensive Survey on Multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08826): Surveys multimodal RAG architectures, retrieval sources, and robustness issues for grounding outputs across input modalities.
