# 3.2.3 Bench

- [APE: Argument Pair Extraction from Peer Review and Rebuttal via Multi-task Learning](https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.569): APE annotates 4,764 peer-review and author-rebuttal passage pairs, then evaluates argument sequence labeling and cross-document relation classification jointly, turning whether a rebuttal addresses a review point into a reproducible scholarly-response extraction task.
- [MReD: A Meta-Review Dataset for Structure-Controllable Text Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07474): Introduces 7,089 meta-reviews and 45K sentences labeled into nine rhetorical categories, then tests extractive and abstractive generation conditioned on review structure.
- [How to Evaluate the Carbon Footprint of Digital Libraries Services: The Case of Citation Verification](https://doi.org/10.1109/jcdl67857.2025.00019): This study evaluates two AI citation-verification implementations—fine-tuned binary classification and sentence-embedding cosine similarity—using measured transformer energy, hardware-manufacturing emissions, and deployment scenarios. Although carbon accounting is the headline contribution, the controlled comparison directly tests AI's ability to verify scholarly citations and supplies a reproducible reliability-and-cost protocol for that research-integrity step.
- [Combinatorial Scientific Discovery: Finding New Concept Combinations Beyond Link Prediction](https://openreview.net/forum?id=rzSVvTBbG9): The work formalizes combinatorial scientific discovery as predicting meaningful combinations of more than two concepts, extending scientific knowledge-graph modeling beyond pairwise link prediction; it supplies benchmark datasets and initial predictive models for ranking multi-concept research ideas and identifying research white spaces.
- [ASAP-Review: A Dataset for Academic Paper Review Generation](https://doi.org/10.1613/jair.1.12862): ASAP-Review aligns papers with reviews annotated by evaluation aspect, introduces metrics for generated reviews, trains paper-to-review summarizers targeted to those aspects, and releases the data, models, ReviewAdvisor tool, and interactive demo as a reproducible automatic-review testbed.
- [NLPeer: A Unified Resource for the Computational Study of Peer Review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.06651): Builds an ethically sourced, multi-venue corpus of over 5K papers and 11K reviews with parsed paper versions, metadata, and guided-skimming, review-assistance tasks.
- [MOPRD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04972): It is based on PeerJ's multidisciplinary open peer review dataset, which collects metadata, multiple rounds of manuscripts, review comments, meta-review, author rebuttal and editorial decision of 6,578 papers, mainly covering biology, medicine, computer science, environment, chemistry and others; the subject proportions given in the paper are roughly biology 46.7%, medicine 19.7%, computer science 15.7%, environment 8.9%, chemistry 4.4%, others 4.6%. It is more suitable than ICLR / NeurIPS / ACL type data for testing interdisciplinary review generation, meta-review generation, rebuttal generation and editorial decision prediction.
- [ORSUM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14647): Use checklist-guided iterative introspection to merge multiple review comments into meta-review and identify consensus and controversy.
- [Towards Coding Social Science Datasets with Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02177): The study evaluates GPT-3 as a synthetic coder for open-ended social-science datasets, comparing it with human coders and other machine-learning methods across domains and coding procedures. Its reported human-comparable performance makes the comparison protocol relevant to scalable research-data annotation.
- [ARIES: A Corpus of Scientific Paper Edits Made in Response to Peer Reviews](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12587): Releases ARIES, linking reviewer comments to the exact author edits that followed; expert tests show that models miss indirect comment-edit relations and tend to produce superficial revisions.
- [Can Large Language Models Provide Useful Feedback on Research Papers?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01783): Comparing LLM feedback with human reviewer feedback, it is pointed out that the model can find some actionable problems, but it can also miss key technical defects and produce generalized suggestions.
- [MLAgentBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03302) ([open-source code](https://github.com/snap-stanford/MLAgentBench)): Evaluates end-to-end automation of machine-learning research tasks. Core idea: package research tasks, execution environments, and scoring protocols into a reproducible platform, measuring whether agents can actually produce runnable experiments and effective improvements.
- [Reflections on inductive thematic saturation as a potential metric for measuring the validity of an inductive thematic analysis with LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03239): Proposes initial thematic saturation (ITS) for LLM-assisted thematic analysis as the ratio between the slopes of unique-code and cumulative-code growth during initial coding; demonstrations on two differently sized datasets produce corresponding codebooks and assess only this initial-coding component of transactional validity.
- [Large language models that replace human participants can harmfully misportray and flatten identity groups](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01908):Across 3,200 participants and 16 demographic identities, the study shows that LLM substitutes can misportray and flatten identity groups, while identity prompts can essentialize them. It tests inference-time techniques that reduce, but do not eliminate, the harms and gives researchers a concrete validity and ethics check for synthetic participants.
- [Can ChatGPT evaluate research quality?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05519):The study applies ChatGPT-4 to 51 articles using UK REF 2021 significance, rigor, and originality criteria, comparing 15 repeated scoring rounds with the author’s judgments. Individual scores correlate weakly (average r=0.281), while averaged scores reach r=0.509, yet the single-author convenience sample remains too limited for formal evaluation.
- [LLMs as Meta-Reviewers' Assistants: A Case Study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15589):The study tests GPT-3.5, LLaMA2, and PaLM2 as meta-reviewer assistants that transform multiple expert reviews into controlled multi-perspective summaries. It compares prompt levels using the TELeR taxonomy and qualitatively analyzes whether the summaries help a senior reviewer form a holistic recommendation.
- [Benchmarking Data Science Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17168): Introduces DSEval, a lifecycle-level evaluation paradigm for data-science agents, and uses bootstrapped annotation to scale benchmark construction beyond isolated analysis or coding subtasks.
- [CASIMIR: A Corpus of Scientific Articles Enhanced with Multiple Author-Integrated Revisions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00241):CASIMIR contains multiple revised versions of 15,646 OpenReview scientific articles with peer reviews, sentence-level alignments, paragraph locations, extracted edits, and revision intentions. The accompanying evaluation of revision systems and metrics finds that current text-revision metrics may not reflect scientific revision quality.
- [Recommending Missed Citations Identified by Reviewers: A New Task, Dataset and Baselines](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.01873): Defines reviewer-identified missed-citation recommendation and releases CitationR, an expert-labeled full-paper benchmark, together with baseline evaluations and RMCNet's attentive encoder over manuscripts, existing references, and omitted citations.
- [Tapilot-Crossing: Benchmarking and Evolving LLMs Towards Interactive Data Analysis Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05307): Tapilot-Crossing provides 1,024 interactive data-analysis interactions across normal, action, private, and private-action settings, while AIR turns successful histories into an adaptive reflection signal and yields up to 44.5% relative improvement.
- [LimGen: Probing the LLMs for Generating Suggestive Limitations of Research Papers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15529):LimGen introduces Suggestive Limitation Generation, asking models to identify useful limitations of research papers for review and future work. Its ACL Anthology corpus contains 4,068 papers paired with associated limitations and supports systematic comparison of LLM approaches, challenges, and opportunities.
- [AI Knowledge and Reasoning: Emulating Expert Creativity in Scientific Research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04436): Uses 589 studies published after the AI training cutoff across four psychology journals over 28 months, redacts their findings, and asks AI to predict the outcomes for comparison with the reported results; selecting post-cutoff papers is intended to reduce prior-exposure and rote-memorization concerns.
- [Characterizing LLM Abstention Behavior in Science QA with Context Perturbations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12452): Perturbs science-QA contexts to test whether models abstain when evidence is missing or wrong, measuring uncertainty handling rather than ordinary answer accuracy.
- [SciFIBench: Benchmarking Large Multimodal Models for Scientific Figure Interpretation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.08807): SciFIBench contains 2,000 human-verified questions over figures and captions from arXiv papers, spanning eight scientific-figure categories and using adversarial filtering to test interpretation, alignment, and reasoning faithfulness.
- [Large Language Models Reflect Human Citation Patterns with a Heightened Citation Bias](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15739):The study asks GPT-4, GPT-4o, and Claude 3.5 to complete anonymized citations in AAAI, NeurIPS, ICML, and ICLR papers, finding human-like patterns with stronger high-citation bias, including among nonexistent references.
- [Re3: A Holistic Framework and Dataset for Modeling Collaborative Document Revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00197):Re3-Sci aligns scientific-paper revisions with peer reviews, author responses, and human edit summaries while labeling revision actions and intentions; the released data, protocols, and code support reproducible collaboration analysis.
- [MASSW: A New Dataset and Benchmark Tasks for AI-Assisted Scientific Workflows](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06357): MASSW turns 152,000 papers from 17 computer-science venues into multi-aspect summaries of literature, ideas, validation, results, and follow-up workflows, making the stages of scientific innovation machine-readable.
- [Use of Large Language Models in the Evaluation of Open-Ended Questionnaire Responses: A Case Study](https://doi.org/10.1109/mipro70003.2026.11591853): Using 3,780 Czech and Slovak respondents and five open-ended questions, the study compares NotebookLM, ChatGPT, Gemini, and ChatGPT Plus for automating the otherwise manual evaluation of questionnaire answers; it is a concrete empirical test of LLM-assisted research-data coding.
- [DISCOVERYWORLD: A Virtual Environment for Developing and Evaluating Automated Scientific Discovery Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06769): Provides 120 simulated discovery tasks across eight scientific topics where an agent must hypothesize, run experiments, analyze results, and act on conclusions; scoring separates task completion, scientifically relevant actions, and discovered explanatory knowledge.
- [DCA-Bench: A Benchmark for Dataset Curation Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07275): Evaluates dataset-curation agents on 221 real dataset quality issues from 187 Kaggle datasets, testing whether agents can identify, reason about, and repair curation problems rather than only describe dataset metadata.
- [DiscoveryBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01725) ([open-source code](https://github.com/allenai/discoverybench)): Evaluates data-driven scientific discovery. Core idea: use real scientific data and hypothesis generation/verification workflows to evaluate whether agents can propose and test meaningful scientific conclusions.
- [CiteME: Can Language Models Accurately Cite Scientific Claims?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12861): Tests open-ended citation attribution on 130 human-curated, non-trivial excerpts from recent machine-learning papers; frontier LMs reach only 4.2-18.5% accuracy versus 69.7% for experts, while a GPT-4o CiteAgent that searches and reads papers reaches 35.3%.
- [SciCode](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13168) ([open-source code](https://github.com/scicode-bench/SciCode); [leaderboard](https://scicode-bench.github.io/leaderboard/)): Evaluates scientific coding for real research problems. Core idea: problems are scientist-curated, provide gold solutions/tests, and decompose research problems into subproblems requiring knowledge recall, reasoning, and code synthesis.
- [Every Part Matters: Integrity Verification of Scientific Figures Based on Multimodal Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18626): Defines Figure Integrity Verification and the Figure-seg dataset for checking whether claims in scientific text align with individual elements of complex figures; its MLLM uses incremental alignment and analogical reasoning rather than coarse chart captioning.
- [SciPostLayout: A Dataset for Layout Analysis and Layout Generation of Scientific Posters](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19787): Releases SciPostLayout, pairing scientific posters with panel-level layout annotations for layout analysis and generation, and evaluates structural prediction beyond plain visual similarity.
- [Evaluating research quality with Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06752): Compares LLM judgments of academic article quality under different inputs and prompting settings, making national evaluation-style research-quality assessment a benchmarkable scoring problem.
- [AI-Driven Review Systems: Evaluating LLMs in Scalable and Bias-Aware Academic Reviews](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10365): Evaluates automated paper-review systems with pairwise human-preference arenas, LLM-assisted review judging, artificial paper errors, adaptive review questions, and venue-specific reviewing materials, making bias and review-quality diagnostics part of the benchmark.
- [Can LLMs Generate Novel Research Ideas?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04109): Evaluates research-idea generation through a large human study with NLP researchers. Core idea: compare expert-written and LLM-generated ideas under blind review, finding stronger perceived novelty but weaker feasibility while exposing self-evaluation and diversity gaps in ideation agents.
- [Can Large Language Models Unlock Novel Scientific Research Ideas?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.06185): Defines Idea Alignment Score and Idea Distinctness Index for paper-conditioned scientific ideation, then validates them against expert judgments of novelty, relevance, and feasibility so idea generation need not rely only on costly manual review.
- [DSBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07703): Input long contextual task descriptions, multi-modal backgrounds, large files and multi-table data, and output data analysis answers, charts, code or modeling results.
- [Benchmarking LLMs in Political Content Text-Annotation: Proof-of-Concept with Toxicity and Incivility Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09741): Uses a protest-event dataset with more than three million digital interactions and a human-coded gold standard for toxicity and incivility to compare API-based GPTs and Perspective with locally deployed open-source models; Perspective at a laxer threshold, GPT-4o, and Nous Hermes 2 Mixtral lead the zero-shot comparison, while only the local open-source LLMs ensure full reproducibility.
- [CORE-Bench / CORE-Bench Hard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11363) ([CORE-Bench Hard](https://hal.cs.princeton.edu/corebench_hard)): Evaluates computational reproducibility of published research. Core idea: convert papers, code, data, and execution environments into reproduction tasks operated by agents, serving as an important baseline for the "research reproduction" line before PaperBench; HAL separately maintains CORE-Bench Hard as an entry for a harder reproducibility subset.
- [Evaluating and Enhancing Large Language Models for Novelty Assessment in Scholarly Publications](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16605): SchNovel tests whether models can rank the novelty of 15,000 temporally ordered arXiv paper pairs across six fields; its RAG-Novelty baseline retrieves comparable papers to make novelty judgments closer to peer review.
- [Pap2Pat: Benchmarking Outline-Guided Long-Text Patent Generation with Patent-Paper Pairs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07009): Defines outline-guided long-text patent generation over paired patent-paper examples, making technology-transfer writing testable with a research-origin input and a patent-form output.
- [MLE-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07095) ([open-source code](https://github.com/openai/mle-bench)): Evaluates machine-learning engineering and data-science competition modeling across 75 Kaggle competitions, scored by competition metrics after generating `submission.csv` and aggregated with `Any Medal`. Core idea: put data loading, EDA, feature engineering, model selection, tuning, training, validation, submission-format repair, and long experiment iteration from real ML engineering into one executable benchmark; the official release also provides a 22-task Low/Lite split to reduce the 3.3TB data and 24-hour run cost of full evaluation.
- [DA-Code: Agent Data Science Code Generation Benchmark for Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07331): Places agents in executable real-data analysis tasks that require planning, data wrangling, and code execution; its dedicated baseline reaches only 30.5% accuracy.
- [Which Demographics do LLMs Default to During Annotation?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08820): On POPQUORN politeness and offensiveness labels, compares unconditioned and irrelevant-placebo prompts with prompts conditioned on age, gender, or race to test which human annotator attributes LLMs mimic by default; demographic prompting produces notable gender, race, and age effects, contrary to earlier reports.
- [Assessing the societal influence of academic research with ChatGPT: Impact case study evaluations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.19948):The study feeds 6,220 REF2021 impact case studies and evaluation guidance to ChatGPT 4o-mini, then compares its scores with departmental expert averages across 34 units. Correlations range from 0.18 to 0.56 at unit level and reach 0.71 for one department, quantifying where LLM impact assessment can support or cross-check reviewers.
- [AAAR-1.0: Assessing AI's Potential to Assist Research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22394): Evaluates LLM support for expertise-intensive researcher tasks: equation inference from paper context, experiment design, paper-weakness detection, and review-critique labeling.
- [AI Interviews the Interviewers: Practitioner Experience and Evaluation of Conversational AI Interviewing](https://doi.org/10.1145/3816046.3816218): Evaluates AI-led qualitative interviewing through a dual-stance study of 14 UX interview practitioners who were interviewed by voice AI and by a human researcher; the transcript analysis shows that paraphrasing, summarizing, adaptive follow-ups, non-formulaic evidence of understanding, and explicit topic, participant, phase, and oversight conditions are central to judging the method.
- [IdeaBench: Benchmarking Large Language Models for Research Idea Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02429): Evaluates LLM research-idea generation from influential papers. Core idea: give models paper titles, abstracts, and references while profiling them as domain researchers, then use GPT-4o ranking over novelty, feasibility, and related criteria to compute an Insight Score.
- [RE-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15114) ([open-source code](https://github.com/METR/RE-Bench)): Evaluates real research-engineering tasks in AI R&D automation, covering open-ended R&D work such as algorithm design, experiment execution, and result analysis. Core idea: complement MLE-bench's Kaggle competition protocol by emphasizing research-engineer-style problem solving, code experiments, and research judgment.
- [Using Machine Learning BERT Models for Plagiarism Detection: A Comprehensive Performance Analysis](https://doi.org/10.1109/impact68503.2026.11468327): A multi-dataset evaluation framework compares BERT, RoBERTa, DeBERTa, ELECTRA, and DistilBERT for semantic plagiarism detection across PAN, AI-generated, multilingual, and paraphrase corpora using a broad set of classification metrics.
- [Are We There Yet? Revealing the Risks of Utilizing Large Language Models in Scholarly Peer Review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01708): Evaluates risks of LLM use in scholarly peer review, focusing on bias, hallucination, score reliability, and process integrity rather than only review fluency.
- [AutoDCWorkflow: LLM-based Data Cleaning Workflow Auto-Generation and Benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06724):AutoDCWorkflow maps a raw table and an analysis purpose to a minimal sequence of six OpenRefine cleaning operations. Its 96-table, 142-purpose benchmark separately scores whether the answer is correct, values match ground truth, and operations resemble human workflows; Llama 3.1, Mistral, and Gemma 2 improve data quality over the baseline.
- [Generative Content Analysis for Policy Research: Comparing LLM Reliability in Analyzing Institutional AI Discourse](https://doi.org/10.1109/cscs66924.2025.00094): Benchmarks GPT-4o and Gemini 2.0 as qualitative-research assistants by independently coding and extracting evidence from AI policies at 33 universities against ten AI-competence vocabularies; inter-coder reliability and output comparisons expose category-dependent agreement and a depth-versus-coverage trade-off between the models.
- [Using Generative AI for Qualitative Coding](https://doi.org/10.1353/lib.2025.a961193): Compares ChatGPT-4 with a human research team on first-level coding, second-level coding, and thematic analysis of graduate-student interviews; it finds useful first-level coding but insufficient reliability as the primary second-level coder, motivating narrower roles such as triangulation, peer debriefing, and bias checks.
- [Do Large Language Models Speak Scientific Workflows?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10606):Evaluates open and closed LLMs on configuring, annotating, and translating workflows across three scientific-workflow experiments and multiple workflow systems; performance varies by task and system, and the models often struggle because scientific-workflow training data is scarce.
- [LiveIdeaBench: Evaluating LLMs' Scientific Creativity and Idea Generation with Minimal Context](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17596): Evaluates scientific idea generation from single-keyword prompts across 1,180 keywords and 22 domains, scoring originality, feasibility, fluency, flexibility, and clarity to test scientific divergent thinking under minimal context.
- [BoxingGym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01540): What it evaluates: automated experimental design and model discovery. Core idea: give agents interactive probabilistic environments, score experimental informativeness with expected information gain, and evaluate whether discovered explanations support later prediction.
- [Benchmarking AI-Generated Text Detection Tools in Academic Writing: An Experimental Evaluation](https://doi.org/10.1109/ichora69329.2026.11537020): This benchmark compares five AI-text detectors on 180 human, generated, and hybrid academic samples using accuracy, F1, and false-positive rate; the sharp hybrid-text degradation and 15.6% to 30.6% false-positive range expose concrete limits for academic-integrity enforcement.
- [Promises and Risks in using LLMs in Scientific Review](https://doi.org/10.1109/percomworkshops68308.2026.11585353): The study generates LLM reviews for the authors' previously published papers, compares them with original human-reviewer feedback, and analyzes agreement, disagreement, benefits, limitations, and responsible-use conditions for peer review.
- [Comparison of algorithms for the recognition of ChatGPT paraphrased texts](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40537-025-01082-0): Builds English and Serbian doctoral-abstract corpora labeled as human-written or ChatGPT-paraphrased, compares 19 word- and character-feature classifiers with ZeroGPT, and reveals a substantial cross-language accuracy gap and temperature-sensitive syntactic shifts.
- [Enhancing Academic Decision-Making: A Pilot Study of AI-Supported Journal Selection in Higher Education](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10755-025-09791-3): Compares LLM journal recommendations with expert judgments for 40 manuscripts across law, psychology, exact sciences, and engineering, finding stronger performance under stable publishing norms and failures on interdisciplinary, niche, emerging, and region-sensitive cases.
- [Aspect-Guided Multi-Level Perturbation Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12510): Evaluates robustness of automated peer review under aspect-guided perturbations. Core idea: perturb papers, reviews, and rebuttals across contribution, soundness, presentation, tone, and completeness to diagnose whether LLM reviewers and meta-reviewers are biased by manipulated evidence.
- [DataSciBench: An LLM Agent Benchmark for Data Science](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13897): Evaluates data-science agents on natural, uncertain prompts with task-function-code structure and validated metrics.
- [Evaluating Sakana's AI Scientist for Autonomous Research: Wishful Thinking or an Emerging Reality Towards 'Artificial Research Intelligence' (ARI)?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14297): Evaluates an autonomous research system. Core idea: test whether AI Scientist-style systems can produce credible research artifacts rather than only optimize local subtasks.
- [MLGym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14499) ([open-source code](https://github.com/facebookresearch/MLGym)): Evaluates interactive ML research environments and benchmarks for AI research agents. Core idea: turn open-ended machine-learning research tasks into Gym-style environments so agents can repeatedly experiment, observe results, and improve research plans.
- [AIRepr: An Analyst-Inspector Framework for Evaluating Reproducibility of LLMs in Data Science](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16395): AIRepr evaluates reproducibility at the workflow level: an Analyst produces executable data-science code and an Inspector checks whether the underlying plan supports the claimed result, rather than judging code only by successful execution.
- [Mind the Blind Spots](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17086): Evaluates LLM-generated peer reviews at the focus/facet level. Core idea: compare attention distributions over review facets against human-review priorities so review quality is judged beyond surface overlap or generic helpfulness.
- [Is Your Paper Being Reviewed by an LLM?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19614): Benchmarks AI-text detection in peer review. Core idea: build peer-review-specific detection resources so academic-integrity evaluation covers whether LLM-written reviews can be distinguished in the domain where the harm occurs.
- [EAIRA: Establishing a Methodology for Evaluating AI Models as Scientific Research Assistants](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20309): Proposes a four-part evaluation methodology for scientific research assistants. Core idea: combine multiple-choice factual tests, open-response reasoning tasks, controlled lab-style experiments, and field-style researcher interactions so model assessment covers both domain knowledge and real scientific use.
- [Relative Neighbor Density](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01508): Provides a domain-agnostic novelty-assessment metric for AI scientist idea evaluation. Core idea: compare an idea's local density against neighboring densities and create scalable test sets without expert labels, giving research-ideation systems a reusable novelty signal.
- [MCiteBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02589): MCiteBench evaluates whether multimodal models ground citations in academic papers and review-rebuttal exchanges, revealing modality bias and unreliable source use that text-only citation benchmarks cannot observe.
- [Codebook Reduction and Saturation: Novel observations on Inductive Thematic Saturation for Large Language Models and initial coding in Thematic Analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04859): Proposes an Inductive Thematic Saturation measure for LLM-assisted initial coding, using a DSPy-based procedure to quantify codebook reduction and saturation in qualitative analysis.
- [Automatic Evaluation Metrics for Artificially Generated Scientific Research](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05712): Studies reusable metrics for evaluating AI-generated research. Core idea: compare citation-count prediction and review-score prediction over OpenReview-derived paper metadata and hypotheses, exposing where proxy-review metrics can and cannot replace expert review.
- [Llms, Virtual Users, and Bias: Predicting Any Survey Question Without Human Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.16498): On World Values Survey questions, GPT-4o, GPT-3.5, Claude, Llama, and Mistral can match aggregate human-response prediction without extra training data, but religious and demographic errors remain substantial; removing censorship improves accuracy for underrepresented groups, while data-trained Random Forests win when sufficient labeled data is available.
- [ResearchBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21248): Evaluates scientific-discovery sub-tasks for LLMs. Core idea: decompose hypothesis discovery into inspiration retrieval, hypothesis composition, and hypothesis ranking using recent papers to reduce contamination.
- [CLAIMCHECK](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21717): Evaluates whether LLM critiques of scientific papers are grounded in the claims they challenge. Core idea: map review weaknesses to disputed paper claims, fine-grained labels, rewrites, and grounded verification so claim-level critique can be judged beyond generic review fluency.
- [Why Stop at One Error? Benchmarking LLMs as Data Science Code Debuggers for Multi-Hop and Multi-Bug Errors](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22388): Evaluates multi-hop error tracing and multi-bug repair in data-science code with 1,117 runtime-debugging cases and 741 annotated cause-effect pairs.
- [SciReplicate-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00255) ([open-source code](https://github.com/xyzCS/SciReplicate-Bench)): Evaluates algorithm reproduction from recent research papers. Core idea: require agents to understand algorithm descriptions in papers, retrieve dependencies, and write executable code, jointly measuring research reproduction ability with reasoning graph accuracy and execution accuracy.
- [PaperBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01848) ([official results](https://github.com/openai/frontier-evals/blob/main/project/paperbench/README.md)): Evaluates reading papers and reproducing experiments/implementations in a fresh sandbox, followed by rubric grading. Core idea: treat "can actually reproduce/run it" as the core acceptance criterion, emphasizing sandbox reruns and rubric judges rather than superficial similarity of generated code.
- [CO-Bench: Benchmarking Language Model Agents in Algorithm Search for Combinatorial Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04310) ([open-source code](https://github.com/sunnweiwei/CO-Bench)): Evaluates agentic algorithm search for combinatorial optimization. Core idea: package 36 real-world CO problems with structured formulations and curated data, then compare LLM agents against human-designed algorithms to expose where iterative algorithm discovery still fails.
- [MLRC-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09702) ([Leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/spaces/launch/MLRC_Bench)): Evaluates agent performance on machine-learning research challenges from more open ML research-challenge scenarios. Core idea: push "can complete competition modeling" further toward "can advance research challenges," making it a transitional evaluation between MLE-bench and MLR-Bench.
- [LLM-SRBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10415): Input cross-domain equation discovery data and transformed physical models, output symbolic formulas, and use symbolic accuracy to check whether the structure is actually restored rather than memorizing common expressions.
- [ArXivBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10496): Evaluates academic-writing assistance through relevant real-paper generation and valid arXiv links across major subject categories, exposing hallucinated citations.
- [Can LLMs Leverage Observational Data? Towards Data-Driven Causal Discovery with LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10936): Evaluates ai support for causal discovery or scientific research workflows.
- [LazyReview](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11042): Evaluates lazy-thinking patterns in NLP peer reviews. Core idea: annotate peer-review sentences with fine-grained lazy-thinking categories so review-assistance systems can be tested for whether they detect superficial or heuristic feedback.
- [HypoBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11524) ([project page](https://chicagohai.github.io/HypoBench/); [open-source code](https://github.com/ChicagoHAI/HypoBench-code); [real leaderboard](https://chicagohai.github.io/HypoBench/leaderboard_real); [synthetic leaderboard](https://chicagohai.github.io/HypoBench/leaderboard_synth)): Evaluates hypothesis generation across real-world and synthetic datasets. Core idea: ask models to propose natural-language explanations for observed phenomena, then score explanatory utility with downstream accuracy/F1 and real-data interestingness dimensions such as novelty, plausibility, and clarity.

AI coding reports a fourfold efficiency gain and a fifteenfold throughput-time advantage, while a hybrid of ChatGPT and domain experts achieves higher inter-coder reliability.

Only 10 of 72 configurations match or exceed human SOTA, while 96.8% of agent methods remain inside human-derived spaces and nearly half exactly match a human algorithm.

Across 29 configurations, the mean normalized score is 0.166 and the best is 0.250, exposing how rarely agents change how models learn.
